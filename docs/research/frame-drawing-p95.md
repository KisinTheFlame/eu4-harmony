# Reducing P95 of the interval between submitted frames

This note investigates where to look for a lower P95 of the interval between two successful frame submissions in the 1.37.5 baseline, and which changes could shorten drawing or its surrounding waits. That interval is the owner's stated target; native draw duration alone is only one possible contributor. The framework's drawing thread and frame preparation use the findings. It reviews the framework source at `8d5358f1`, the repository's first-hand disassembly, three new run records and focused offline disassembly on 2026-11-14. No runtime optimization was applied. Every address is an RVA into the baseline. Current performance-window definitions and measurements are separate: [performance.md](../performance.md) and [performance-boundaries.md](performance-boundaries.md).

The subsequent [P99 investigation](frame-drawing-p99.md) directly attributes individual long submission intervals and verifies an excessive lock-order ownership lifetime. The candidates below remain historical P95 leads; they do not establish what caused the 8–10 ms P95 region.

## Current performance-window results

The [default run](../prototype-log/2026-11-14-frame-p95-baseline.md), [first camera comparison](../prototype-log/2026-11-14-frame-p95-camera.md) and [repeat](../prototype-log/2026-11-14-frame-p95-repeat.md) retain their separate setups and limits. The first two overlapped a Ghidra whole-program import, which timed out and was closed. The last ran after that analysis stopped, but another game was present. These are complete instrumented hidden-game measurements on a shared machine, not controlled optimization comparisons.

| First run, speed 5 for 120 s | 9.911 / 31.249 / 168.426 | 6.309 / 29.390 |
| Repeat, paused fixed 1 / 2, 24 s each | 7.333 / 7.671 / 11.004; 7.533 / 8.221 / 39.445 | 6.196 / 9.522; 6.270 / 36.947 |

All eleven camera-comparison/repeat windows and both default-run windows reached their deadlines with no lost records or failed submissions. Moving 1 in the repeat had a Present disturbance that the second moving window did not reproduce; system load was not fixed. The warmed paused route does not establish a large stable map-drawing penalty. It also does not cover the late-game moving-camera case in the older research.

The two speed-5 windows recorded 70 and 73 completed gaps over 50 ms, and 14 and 13 over 100 ms. Their time-weighted interface-age P95s were 63.424 and 62.237 ms; day rates were 929 and 942 completed days/min. The high frame-submission rate therefore does not imply equally fresh interface content. Maxima show that some long gaps occur largely outside the ending Present call. P95s cannot be subtracted to identify how much of a particular slow interval was waiting.

**Priority:** investigate the 8–10 ms submission intervals at their shared-state and preparation boundaries first, then test a narrow change to the work actually occupying those intervals. Message-popup invalidation is a particularly clear lead for longer freezes; shortening repeated preparation is a concrete source-level candidate for ordinary lock occupancy. Their effect on P95 is not yet measured. Broad renderer replacement, reduced visual quality, removing the lock, or turning off vertical sync are not established fixes. Framework limiting was already off, while vsync was enabled; an unlimited framework loop does not remove waiting inside Present.

## What the evidence establishes

The best existing lead for work inside drawing is the map while the camera moves. In the [2026-11-11 run](../prototype-log/2026-11-11-late-frames-by-part.md), paused drawing averaged 3.1 ms with a still camera and 5.4–13.3 ms with a moving camera; the longest draws were 9.1 ms and 38–118 ms respectively. At speed 5 with a moving camera, 406 of 523 running drawing-thread samples were under the 3D map, with the scene passes and `d3d9.dll` prominent. **These are older averages, maxima and whole-run samples, not a current P95 decomposition.** Two other test games were running and computer load was 70–100%; the run did not verify a quiet computer or a shown game. It supports making camera movement a controlled variable, not assigning a fraction of today's tail to any function.

The game's drawing has distinct work with different reasons to run:

| Work | Existing first-hand evidence | Consequence for a speed-up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Province texture rebuilds and names | [Map drawing reads](map-drawing-reads.md), off-screen passes and names; current [prepare.rs](../../core/src/drawing/prepare.rs), `frame`, `passes`, `make_names` | Rebuilds consume inputs the day writes. The framework does them during main-thread preparation; the drawing thread receives clear rebuild flags. Making this work cheaper primarily shortens preparation and lock occupancy. |
| Camera-dependent terrain and trees | [Map drawing reads](map-drawing-reads.md), off-screen passes: `0x12dab90` reaches `0x12e8c00`, `0x12e5d30` and `0x12dac90` according to settings, independently of the rebuild flags | These still run in the drawing thread. They are candidates for measuring motion-related work. The existing note does not prove that their callees do expensive work on every invocation. |
| Shadow and scene passes | [Map drawing reads](map-drawing-reads.md), the 3D map: `0x10a55c0`, `0x1540c70`, `0x14dcde0`, `0x1540610` and the other scene passes | The scene is submitted for several views, with mesh-part selection and matrix copies. The shadow interpretation is marked inferred in that note. A draw-call or visibility speed-up needs the actual work of each pass established. |

Caching by camera alone would miss other inputs and can leave wrong outlines or visibility. |
| GUI drawing | [GUI drawing](gui-drawing.md), the walk and read/write inventory | It writes transforms and hover lists, and some classes load textures or write buffers. Reusing a whole GUI draw is not a pure replay of immutable commands. |

These locations are search targets, not a claim that any is presently responsible for the slowest 5% of draws.

## Candidate: shorten invalidation and presentation-lock occupancy

The current [loaded baseline](../prototype-log/2026-11-14-frame-p95-baseline.md) makes shared-state coordination a stronger lead than optimizing all native drawing indiscriminately. Its twelve drawing-log buckets contained 109 held-back late drawings, 119 attributed to a lock before drawing or after Present, and only two attributed to drawing itself. These are clues from a different endpoint and a threshold above the measured P95, not attribution of the slowest 5% of successful submissions.

The source establishes two distinct stops:

1. A crossing invalidates the prepared frame. During a day it also takes the presentation lock, which stays held until the next in-day frame check. Releasing that lock does not make the frame valid again; a new preparation must finish first ([turns.rs](../../core/src/drawing/turns.rs), `MainSide::crossing`, `frame_check`; [crossings.rs](../../core/src/drawing/crossings.rs), `crossing`, `frame_prepared`; [course.rs](../../core/src/drawing/course.rs), `CrossingsTracker`).
2. Present runs without the presentation lock. Its successful return is already recorded when the drawing thread tries to take the lock again. A long wait there delays the next submission, although it is outside the current Present-call duration ([turns.rs](../../core/src/drawing/turns.rs), `DrawingSide::present_begins`, `present_ends`; [performance-boundaries.md](performance-boundaries.md)).

Message popups (`0x8c1430`), monthly interface refresh (`0x81e280`) and event activation (`0x828c00`) appear in the new logs. The popup factory builds several different dialog classes, the message handler posts the result, and a popup copies both its message and its action callback ([message-pop-up-actions.md](message-pop-up-actions.md), construction). A deferred implementation therefore cannot just enqueue the factory's three raw argument pointers or return a null window: it must preserve the result expected by the caller, ownership of the message and callback, ordering, and pause/action behavior. Those are conditions on an optimization, not a completed design. Event activation already has a correctness history: removing its crossing let drawing meet partially initialized option textures ([drawing-beside-the-day.md](drawing-beside-the-day.md), an event's window met half made).

A focused full-body disassembly of the popup factory after the measurements confirmed the argument-lifetime obstacle directly: all recognized kinds build a dialog, then consume message/action temporaries before returning the dialog pointer. The common cleanup at `0x8c1ac2` destroys the message's text and another owned member; the action path at `0x8c1ad5` calls its implementation's `+0x20` disposal method and clears the implementation pointer. The unrecognized-kind path at `0x8c1953` also cleans up and returns null. This matches the older constructor study, but does not establish a safe deferred replacement for the handler and its callees. It rules out retaining the existing argument pointers for later use. The read used the installed baseline and the already-built repository disassembler; it did not execute the factory or measure its cost.

The useful direction is to reduce how much unrelated simulation remains inside a crossing's lock hold, or to prepare/publish a complete interface change at a safe boundary. Moving suitable owned display work to the existing preparation queue may shorten the interval of invalidity. An earlier complete preparation may shorten it too, but more preparations can cost simulation throughput. Neither change is justified by the crossing's name alone: the code that completes the window, any writes after the apparent return, and every relevant consumer must be understood first. Unlocking at the popup factory's return is not equivalent to publishing a valid frame.

For P95 specifically, the first diagnostic must cover the intervals around the observed 8–10 ms shoulder as well as the much rarer 50–100 ms gaps. The existing late-drawing threshold is about 13.89 ms at the detected 144 Hz rate, even when framework limiting is disabled. Its log cannot decide which stage dominates those 8–10 ms intervals. Record an individual interval's previous submission return, lock waits, preparation/invalidation transitions, pre-Present work and next successful return on one clock; associate the crossing and update identifiers. This can be a one-off investigation before committing to a lasting developer-tool interface. A stage histogram alone would still lose the needed association.

## Candidate: separate rebuilds from camera work

There is a concrete duplication of invocation in the current source:

1. Main-thread `frame_draws` calls `prepare::frame` before publishing a frame ([drawing.rs](../../core/src/drawing.rs), `frame_draws`).
2. `prepare::frame` calls the full off-screen-pass function, `0x10a51e0`, with the real rebuild flags at every preparation ([prepare.rs](../../core/src/drawing/prepare.rs), `passes`).
3. The drawing thread subsequently calls the same native function with clear rebuild flags. Those flags suppress the rebuilds, but not the camera-dependent part ([prepare.rs](../../core/src/drawing/prepare.rs), `passes_asked_stub`; [map-drawing-reads.md](map-drawing-reads.md), off-screen passes).

**Hypothesis:** separating the rebuild-only preparation from the camera-dependent work may remove work from the main thread's locked preparation and leave the drawing thread to prepare the camera-dependent part once for the view it actually draws. This is a particularly useful candidate if slow submission intervals correlate with preparation or lock waits.

The focused static reading below confirms that the two CPU preparation functions do not cache an unchanged camera: they really repeat their loops. The two invocations need not have the same camera, however. The drawing thread moves the camera before drawing, while preparation uses the camera available then ([drawing.rs](../../core/src/drawing.rs), `run`, `move_camera`). Their output consumers also matter. Removing a whole call because its rebuild flags are clear is not justified.

### The native boundary, read to the returns on 2026-11-14

Primary source: the installed `eu4.exe`, SHA-256 `9ad3efe1af169f40ee577f9dae5debbc87af6fb8b5450fb345ebf110dc4d771a`, read with the repository's already-built `disasm` example after `cargo xtask what` for each function. The complete bodies of `0x10a51e0`, `0x12dab90`, `0x12e8c00`, `0x12e5d30`, `0x12dac90`, and the small helpers `0x8023b0`, `0x8023d0`, `0xca250`, `0x12e7f10`, `0x416ef0` were read. Selected consumers were followed to their reads of these outputs; their entire transitive graphics call graphs were not established. REA's initial Ghidra query timed out during its 330-second full-program import and produced no function body; it was closed before the later test repeat. These findings are from the subsequent local disassembly, not from successful REA decompilation or execution.

**There is a real split in the native wrapper.** In `0x12dab90`, the code before `0x12dac1d` performs the province-texture, tree-layer and final flagged rebuilds. At `0x12dac1d` it loads settings and begins a distinct suffix: trees when settings `+0x13b` is set, terrain when `+0x138` is set, and the off-screen render target when both `+0x139` and `+0x138` are set. The suffix ends at the common epilogue `0x12dac7a`. The outer `0x10a51e0` clears the flags and updates the names-due/fade fields *after* this call returns. A preparation-only bypass of that suffix, rather than bypassing the outer function, could therefore retain the existing rebuild and flag lifecycle. This is a candidate intervention boundary, not yet a safety proof for all later readers.

| Function and output | Inputs and exact work observed | Reuse boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tree camera preparation, `0x12e8c00`, output at tree object `+0x878`, eight bytes per cell | Divides map width and height into a grid based on 256-pixel cells. For each cell it initializes the output view byte to zero and distance to the largest finite float, tests up to three camera views against four planes, and retains the passing view with the nearest squared distance. The view byte is replaced when a nearer view wins; it is not the union of all visible views. It reads map dimensions and the camera, not province or country objects. | No unchanged-camera test or cached-result branch exists. With identical map dimensions, view-enable flags, view planes and view positions, its arithmetic has identical inputs. Reusing the arrays also needs the same tree object and proof that a rebuild or other writer has not replaced the results. |
| Terrain camera preparation, `0x12e5d30`, output vectors at terrain object `+0x68` and `+0x80` | Computes per-cell squared distance from camera position, including the two enabled horizontally wrapped views; forms successive 2-by-2 minimum-distance levels on the stack; selects grid sizes from fixed thresholds; classifies visibility for three views; writes a two-byte size/view record per cell and a byte of neighboring-size edge flags. `+0xc4` is the base grid size and `+0xc8` controls levels. It reads map dimensions, camera position/planes/enable flags, object geometry settings and constant thresholds. | No unchanged-camera early return. The output vectors are initialized/resized when empty, not allocated afresh unconditionally. Reuse requires all camera and geometry inputs, output storage identity and invalidation on replacement; a prepared-frame sequence alone is insufficient because the drawing thread moves its camera within that sequence. |
| Off-screen rendering, `0x12dac90`, render target at pdxmap `+0x16b8` | Lazily creates a render target if missing; unbinds texture slots, saves and sets the target, begins a scene, clears it, uploads each active view's constants, and calls terrain drawing. Under an additional setting/height test it transforms a copy of the view and draws a scene pass. It ends the scene and restores the target. | This is actual rendering, not just camera arithmetic. It depends on renderer state, resources and potentially the scene's objects, as well as the camera. Unchanged camera does not establish unchanged output. Do not apply the CPU functions' reuse argument to this whole function. |

The tree helper calls were read: `0x8023b0` and `0x8023d0` only test the camera's wrapping-view enable bytes (`+0x778` and `+0x678`/`+0x679`); `0xca250` only returns a stack wrapper around its pointer argument. The tree preparation therefore makes no device call or world-state call through its direct helpers. The terrain function's remaining direct calls are stack probing, memory filling and vector growth/resize helpers: `0x12e7f10` grows and zero-fills the two-byte output vector, and `0x416ef0` sizes the byte vector. Allocator and failure routines below those helpers were not re-read; the loops themselves issue no graphics-back-end call. The off-screen rendering function, in contrast, explicitly reaches the back-end table and the scene pass `0x1540610`, so its full write set cannot be inferred from the CPU preparation's.

**Output consumers found.** Tree drawing `0x12e9130` reads each cell's distance and view byte at `0x12e92ad` and `0x12e92c9`, then uses them to choose the entries for the requested view. Terrain drawing `0x12e6890`, at `0x12e6d70` through `0x12e6da4`, reads the size/view vector and the edge byte, rejects unselected cells, and uses the selected grid size and edge flags in the draw constants and index ranges. The main pdxmap draw `0x12db0c0` calls these for its views at `0x12db2c7` and `0x12db2f6`. Crucially, the off-screen suffix itself calls terrain drawing at `0x12daf9e`: omitting only the main-thread terrain calculation while leaving that main-thread rendering in place would feed it the previous calculation. A coherent split must keep that producer and consumer together, or reuse results only when the exact inputs match.

**What is actionable, and what remains open.** There are two bounded candidates: omit the entire camera suffix during main-thread preparation while retaining it immediately before drawing; or keep both call sites but reuse only the CPU preparation outputs when every input is unchanged. The second avoids changing the relative order of the off-screen render pass, but still needs storage/writer invalidation. The first removes a repeated render pass as well, but needs an audit of intervening consumers after preparation, especially the names-building call, and of the off-screen target's other readers and the optional scene pass's side effects. Those readers are not proved absent by this limited check; the names-building function's transitive call graph, indirect readers of the arrays/target, and the scene pass's full writes were outside this focused reading. No code change is safe to ship on the entry-point split alone.

Time rebuild and camera portions separately on both threads before selecting either candidate. Retain the province-vector consumption on the main thread: [map-drawing-reads.md](map-drawing-reads.md) documents that the day appends to the same vector that rebuilding empties. Moving that consumption back to the drawing thread reintroduces a known race. A successful split must preserve map-mode changes, changing province ownership, names, trees and terrain during camera movement, while reducing the measured preparation or draw cost. This note establishes no expected percentage gain.

## Candidate: camera-dependent map and scene work

The moving-camera lead and the native call inventory justify investigating the map before a new rendering architecture. The measured scene-pass samples are inclusive of lower-level rendering, including driver calls; they do not distinguish CPU visibility work, repeated state changes, buffer uploads or waiting inside the graphics stack ([2026-11-11 run](../prototype-log/2026-11-11-late-frames-by-part.md); [map-drawing-reads.md](map-drawing-reads.md), the 3D map).

Investigate these mechanisms separately:

- **Work selected for each view.** Establish whether visibility or mesh-part selection is repeated with identical inputs across the map's views, and what actually changes between them. Reuse needs a key that includes the view and the relevant object state; three camera views are not one equivalent calculation. Existing evidence identifies the passes and lists, but does not prove avoidable repetition ([map-drawing-reads.md](map-drawing-reads.md), the 3D map; [drawing-beside-the-day.md](drawing-beside-the-day.md), the day's crossings read again).
- **Buffer writing and submission.** The game's back end already caches several states, and dynamic buffer writes choose discard or no-overwrite according to flags. Measure calls reaching the device and resource waits before adding another state cache or queue. A cache above a renderer whose state other drawing changes needs complete invalidation ([gui-drawing.md](gui-drawing.md), the back end slots and renderer-cache risks).
- **Resource appearance during movement.** Lazy texture loading is a real draw-time path, and some GUI classes make buffers during drawing. Whether it explains the moving-camera tail is unknown. Compare first exposure of an area with repeated passes over it and identify the resources loaded during slow draws before proposing preloading ([gui-drawing.md](gui-drawing.md), what drawing reads and writes). A rare first exposure may explain a maximum without moving P95.

Changing terrain, trees, shadows, resolution or multisampling one at a time is useful as a diagnostic comparison where the game exposes that setting. A result would establish sensitivity to that setting, not the cause within its implementation. Reducing visible detail is a quality choice, not a demonstrated equivalent speed-up. The baseline and any control must record which settings actually changed.

## Candidate: inexpensive framework work around drawing

The framework source exposes smaller, narrower opportunities. None has a measured contribution to the tail yet.

**Map-icon projection.** `icons::follow_camera` reads all sixteen matrix scalars for every icon, even where icons refer to the same matrix. It calls a window's position setter whenever the new position differs from the position of the *prepared* frame, not from the position last set by this helper. `icons::prepared` takes the placing vector and replaces the old prepared vector, losing the old vector's allocation for subsequent placing ([icons.rs](../../core/src/drawing/icons.rs), `prepared`, `follow_camera`). Candidates are one matrix read per distinct view per draw, retaining staging capacity between preparations, and omitting setters only after establishing every writer and the setter's effects. The first two have narrower semantics than caching whole native drawing. **Metric limitation:** this helper runs before `run` starts its native-drawing timer, so reducing it improves surrounding frame work and may improve submission intervals without lowering that timer's draw duration ([drawing.rs](../../core/src/drawing.rs), `run`).

**Temporary border inputs.** The current implementation already retains its buffers and borrows the prepared country bitset instead of copying it each draw. It still copies live province flags and merges only the temporary highlight bit because the same prepared update can be drawn with changing visibility ([marks.rs](../../core/src/drawing/marks.rs), `capture_into`, `prepared_reader`; [map-drawing-reads.md](map-drawing-reads.md), temporary highlights). There is no evidence to rank this small linear copy above native map work. Caching the whole result by prepared-frame sequence would lose live visibility changes.

**Framework work before native Present.** Font-page growth, the framework UI, late native tooltips and requested screenshot readback happen before the drawing thread releases the presentation lock for native Present ([framework_ui/hook.rs](../../core/src/framework_ui/hook.rs), `present`). An end-to-end slow draw can therefore contain this work. Check whether it ran on slow frames before assigning all pre-Present time to the native map. Screenshots should be taken outside measurement windows unless screenshot cost is the subject. This is a confound to identify, not evidence that the framework UI is presently expensive.

## What not to select from this evidence

**Removing the presentation lock** is not a supported shortcut. The two threads share the renderer's render target, vertex-declaration state, GUI objects and live resources. Earlier runs recorded frames drawn into event-button textures and use of freed mesh data when those accesses crossed ([drawing-beside-the-day.md](drawing-beside-the-day.md), a text drawn into its texture in a day, and the whole scenario set). The current table entries already bypass the Rust crossing handler on non-main threads; assuming a heavyweight crossing callback for every drawing-thread device call would misread [crossings.rs](../../core/src/drawing/crossings.rs), `renderer_entries`.

**Recording the GUI or adding another Direct3D submission thread** is not justified by the existing drawing evidence alone. The recorded-GUI proposal has unresolved transforms, hover, animations, resource lifetime and renderer-cache state ([gui-drawing.md](gui-drawing.md), risks). The older driver investigation measured whole-day shares, on another build and in a hidden game, and could not distinguish a driver-queue wait from a GPU wait ([drawing-beside-the-day.md](drawing-beside-the-day.md), Direct3D calls on a thread of their own). Neither demonstrates a reduction of the current submission-interval P95. GUI recording is a possible response to a proven update/drawing contention problem, which is a separate measured cause.

## Evidence needed to choose the change

For the same saved position and a reproducible camera path, distinguish paused from speed 5, still from moving camera, first exposure from warmed repeated movement, and map drawing from main-thread preparation. Keep the resolution, playset, visible windows, target frame rate and machine load fixed. The older moving-camera profile cannot substitute for this control ([2026-11-11 run](../prototype-log/2026-11-11-late-frames-by-part.md)).

Identify the actual slow frames and attribute *their* durations to preparation/lock waits, camera and icon work, off-screen camera passes, 3D map subpasses, GUI, framework work and native Present as applicable. Whole-run CPU samples can guide where to look, but they do not label slow frames, and a CPU sample in a driver is not a GPU duration. Quantiles of nested or adjacent stages cannot simply be added to recover the quantile of their total.

The recommended first code experiment is conditional: if crossings or lock waits dominate the slow submission intervals, establish what changes or work keep those frames waiting before changing drawing itself; if locked preparation repeats costly camera work, isolate the rebuild-only part; if the long draws themselves are in a scene or terrain pass, reduce the specific measured repeated calculation or submission there. Small framework allocation/read changes are useful only after measuring their contribution. Accept a change only when repeated comparable windows improve the intended tail without shifting the cost into interface update age, completed-day throughput, failed submissions or longer maximum gaps. No candidate in this note has yet passed that test.
