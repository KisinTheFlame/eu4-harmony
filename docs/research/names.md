# People and their names, and battle names (for `wording::names`, `wording::battles` and `game::countries`)

Baseline 1.37.5. All addresses are RVAs. Found by reading eu4.exe and the game's files on 2026-10-28; what was checked in the running game is marked "tested".

## People

- **Rulers, heirs and consorts** share one layout (log strings name it `CMonarch`, monarch.cpp); vtables `0x1cdcd40`/`0x1cdcdd8` (monarch), `0x1c52240`/`0x1cdd0a8` (heir), `0x1c520c0`/`0x1cdd270` (consort-like), `0x1c522f8` (foreign heir).
  - `+0x20` the owner's tag; `+0x28` the given name, a `std::string` **with the regnal number in it** ("Louis XI"; `0x314400` turns `#N` in a name list into the numeral); `+0x58` religion; `+0x60` the person's culture (`CCulture*`); `+0x68` the dynasty, whose name is a `std::string` at `+8`; `+0x74` female; `+0x78` the regency's estate.
  - A country's monarch is at `+0x1280`, heir `+0x1298`, consort through `0x30f660`; its primary culture at `+0x1228`.
- **A country's three persons, read for `country.get`** (2026-11-11, from the country's save writer `0x2dc6b0` and the person's `0xa4a750`, the writer at slot `+0x10` of the vtable `0x1cdcd40`):
  - The country holds a pointer to each: the ruler at `+0x1280` (`monarch`, token `0x2840`, written at `0x2e05a8`), the heir at `+0x1298` (`heir`, `0x2d41`) and the consort at `+0x12b0` (`queen`, `0x343f`; there is no `consort` key in the writer). While it has none, the place holds the game's null person of that kind, made once (`[0x233ffa0]`, `[0x233ffa8]`, `[0x233ffb0]`, as the repair of the histories names them). The writer writes each only when its method at vtable `+0x58` says yes (`0x2e0588` `49 8b 8f 80 12 00 00 48 8b 01 ff 50 58`, and the same for `+0x1298` at `0x2e05b5` and `+0x12b0` at `0x2e05e2`); the base class's slot answers no (`0x8f450`), the heir's and consort's classes' yes (`0x8f440`). What it writes of each is its ID, `+0x10`, through `0x9a920`.
  - `0x30f660`(country) answers the ruler when the ruler's method `+0x60` says yes, else `+0x12b0`: the one the court view shows as the queen.
  - In the person: administrative skill `+0x48` (`ADM`, `0xa4a837` `8b 7e 48`), diplomatic `+0x4c` (`DIP`, `0xa4a7ef` `8b 7e 4c`), military `+0x50` (`MIL`, `0xa4a87f` `8b 7e 50`), whole numbers written with `%d`; the given name `+0x28` (`0xa4a7a3` `48 8d 56 28`); the dynasty `+0x68`, written as `dynasty` only when its method at `+8` says yes, its name at `+8` (`0xa4b159` `48 8b 4e 68 48 8b 01 ff 50 08 84 c0 74 30 48 8b 7e 68 48 83 c7 08`); the birth date `+0x70` (`birth_date`, `0x2d42`; `0xa4b197` `48 8d 4e 70`); its religion through `+0x58` and its culture through `+0x60`.
  - **Age**: `0xa4e2d0`(person) answers it in whole years, the year of the world's date (world `+0x1dd0`) less the year of the birth date (`+0x70`, `0xa4e2ee` `4c 8d 61 70`), one less when the birthday is still to come that year, never below 0; the `heir_age` and `consort_age` scope values (`0x6dfed0`, in the table `0x6a7af0`) call it. A person the game made with no birth date of its own is very old by it: in the 1760 save, Qing's ruler 1759 years, Ming's 131.
  - **Tested** (2026-11-11, [the run](../prototype-log/2026-11-11-country-rulers-legitimacy-modifiers.md)): France's three in 1444 as the court view shows them (Charles VII 4/2/4 aged 41, Louis 4/3/2 aged 21, Marie d'Anjou 4/4/4 aged 40); the Ottomans' regent and heir and no consort in a 1760 save; Henry VI's 0/0/0 and age against his history; Poland's 1444 regency as a person with no given name and 0/0/0.
- **The full name**: `0xa4b2c0`(person, out) puts the given name in out (`0xa4b2df` `48 8d 51 28`), then, when the dynasty is valid, a one-character `" "` (`0xa4b468` `66 c7 45 b8 20 00`) and the dynasty's name (`0xa4b44c` `48 8b 57 68 48 83 c2 08`), then " (regent)" during a regency. So the order "given name, dynasty" is fixed in code.
  - 55 call sites: the court view (`0xed2be0`; the title goes in front, "国王 …"), the ruler, heir and consort tooltips, the `FULLMONARCHNAME` message value, new-ruler and regency messages, the HRE and Celestial Empire views, the front end, and `0xa4baa0`, which copies a ruler's full name into the general he becomes.
  - `0xa4b230` gives the given name only (118 callers), as does `[Root.Monarch.GetName]` in localisation; event texts that want a full name write `[Root.Monarch.GetName] [Root.Monarch.Dynasty.GetName]` themselves.
- **The lines on what follows the ruler's death** (`MDEATH_HEIR_SUCCEEDS`, `MDEATH_REGENCY_RULE`, in the ruler's tooltip, alerts and the diplomacy tooltip) come from `0x318870`, which builds `HEIR_NAME` in place: `" " + dynasty` into `[rsp+0x68]` (`0x3190ff`–`0x31919c`), the given name from `0xa4b230`, then `call append` at `0x319257` / `0x319324` with the heir in `rbx` (`0x3191fa` `48 8b cb`).
- **Generals and advisors** keep one string (`+0x20`), made when they are created by `0x313d90` / `0x313f40`: a given name from the culture's or country's lists, then a family name. It is saved with them, so it cannot be split again reliably: names in the data already mix orders ("Di Zhu", "Yi Sun-Sin", "Wang Yangming"), and family names can hold spaces ("Nguyen Phuc", "Aisin Gioro").

## Picking a name from a list

- **`0xda9880`(list, random, &male factor, &female factor, sex)** picks a name from a list of weighted names (a country's `monarch_names`): entries of `0x28` bytes from `+0x18` to `+0x20`, each with its weight at `+0x20` and a female flag at `+0x24`; the list's total weight is at `+0x10`, that of its male names at `+0x14`. With sex 0 or 1 it takes `random % weight` of that sex's names and walks down them, taking each one's weight off until it goes below 0.
- **Sex 2** weighs both sexes, each by its factor, in fixed point (×1000): the weight is `male weight × 1000 × male factor / 1000 + female weight × 1000 × female factor / 1000`, and the draw is `random × 1000 % weight` (`0xda9981` `imul eax, esi, 0x3e8`). The random number is the game's generator's (`0x14c7420`, a Mersenne Twister at `0x14c71b0`), 0 to 2^31 − 1, so `random × 1000` overflows 32 bits and is negative about half the time; the remainder is then negative, goes below 0 at the first name, and the list's first name is picked about half the time. With the 40 names of Ming's list, weighing 10 each, the first, "Zhanji", is picked 51.5% of the time instead of 2.5% (the generator emulated in Python from its code, 100 000 draws).
- A new heir (`0xa55cf0`, heir.cpp) is named with sex 2 unless its sex is already set, the male factor 1 and the female factor 1 + the country's female heir chance (modifier `0x75`); the name picked gives the heir's sex (`0xa55fc5`), so with the overflow an heir also takes the first name's sex about half the time. `0x3146f0`, which names rulers, passes its own argument as the sex; the caller read (`0x30d4cc`) passes the ruler's female flag, 0 or 1.
- The framework's repair takes the random number modulo the weight as it is (`mov eax, esi`): the remainder is then in [0, weight), as every name's share is its weight's.

## Cultures

- **`CCulture`** (constructor `0x27e810`): `+0x48` its key as in common/cultures (`0x27e8a7` `4c 8d 76 48`), `+0x68` its name from the localisation, `+0x88` its group (stored at `0x27e8fe`, read at `0x280952` `48 8b 91 88 00 00 00`).
- **`CCultureGroup`** (constructor `0x27c9f0`): `+0x38` its key (`0x27ca3c` `48 8d 71 38`), `+0x58` its name from the localisation, `+0x78` its index in file order (match on keys, not indices).
- A person's culture link in localisation scopes reads `+0x60` (`0x280aba` `48 8b 41 60`).
- **East Asian, family name first** (common/cultures/00_cultures.txt): the groups `east_asian` (Chinese cultures, and `vietnamese_new`, `korean_new` from the Empire of China), `korean_g`, `japanese_g`, and the culture `vietnamese`. Not family first although in `east_asian`: `tibetan_new`, `altaic_new`, `zhuang_new`, `manchu_new`.

## Battles

- `0x23a900`(combat, out) is the key's localisation (`BATTLE_OF`, or `SIEGE_OF` for a siege, or `OCCUPATION_OF` where the province has no fort), then `" "` and the province's name: the order is fixed in code. 10 callers: land and naval battle results (`LANDBATTLEOVER`), siege and occupation over, a leader's death in battle, the combat and siege views and their tooltips.
- The war overview's battle row (`0x148a6d0`, through a vtable) builds its own: `BATTLE_OF` + `" §Y"` + the battle's stored name + `"§!"`.
- The Chinese language mod has `BATTLE_OF` "战役", `SIEGE_OF` "围攻", `OCCUPATION_OF` "占领"; the game shows "战役 雅典".
- The localisation lookup is `0x14b34a0`(out, key, values, count), which returns the key when it is missing; `0x95660` destroys a `std::string`.

## What the framework does

- `wording::names` replaces `0xa4b2c0` and the two appends at `0x319257` / `0x319324`. For a person whose culture is East Asian (above), when the player language has the template `EU4HARMONY_NAME_FAMILY_FIRST` (Simplified Chinese: "$FAMILY$ $GIVEN$"), the name becomes family name first, and a space between two Chinese, Japanese or Korean characters is dropped. Generals, advisors and names put together in localisation keep the game's order.
  - Tested 2026-10-28: the lobby shows "皇帝 Zhu Qizhen" for Ming and "国王 Yi Sejong Do" for Korea (the data's given name is "Sejong Do"); Korea's court shows "Yi Sejong Do", "Yi Hyang" and "Sim Soheon"; the ruler's tooltip ends "Yi Hyang继承王位".
- `wording::battles` replaces `0x23a900`: with the player language's templates (`EU4HARMONY_BATTLE`, `_SIEGE`, `_OCCUPATION`, with `$KIND$` and `$PLACE$`; Chinese "雅典战役", "围攻雅典", "占领雅典") the name is reworded; the kind is told by comparing the text with the three keys' localisations. Not yet seen in the running game.
