# Countries (for `game::countries`)

Baseline 1.37.5. All addresses are RVAs. Found by reading eu4.exe on 2026-10-29; what was checked in the running game is marked "tested".

## The country array

- **The country manager** is at the global `0x233d8d0`. Its countries are an array object at `+0x110`: the data, one pointer per country indexed by the tag's index (bits 32–47 of the tag), at `+0x118`; the length, an i32, at `+0x124`. In arrays of this type `+0x10` is the capacity (below), so `+0x120` presumably is; no code reads it through the manager, and the framework does not use it.
  - Indexing, at almost every place that turns a tag into its country, for example `0xb239c` (`4c 8b 05 … 48 8b 41 20 48 c1 e8 20 48 0f bf d0 49 8b 80 18 01 00 00 48 8b 1c d0`): load the manager, shift the tag right by 32, sign-extend the 16-bit index, read `+0x118`, read the pointer at index × 8.
  - The length: when the manager is made (`0xb510c6`, after `new` and the constructor `0x2812a0`), `lea rdx, [rax+0x110]` hands the array to `0xb510f0`, which reads its length at `+0x14` and its data at `+8` (`0xb5113f` `41 8b 46 14 4d 8b 76 08 48 98 49 8d 2c c6`) and walks every pointer, reading each country's tag at `+0x20` (`0xb5117c` `48 8b 5b 20`). The array it fills, in its first argument at `+0x10`, has the same layout: data `+8`, capacity `+0x10` (doubled when the length would pass it, `0xb51176`–`0xb51188`), length `+0x14`.
- **A country's tag** is at `+0x20` of the country (`country::TAG`); its index is the country's place in the array. The framework finds a country by its letters by walking the array to its length and comparing the letters of each valid tag, checking that each tag's index is its place (`game::world::countries`).

What the framework relies on is in `objects::campaign::countries` and the dependency points "country array" (`0xb510c6`) and "country array walk" (`0xb5113f`).

## A country's snapshot (`countries::snapshot`)

How it was found: the game writes a country into a save in `0x2dc6b0` (its argument in `r15`), one field after another, each followed by the token of its key in the binary save format (`mov edx, <token>`). The tokens' names are in a table in `.data`: an u32 token, then its key as text, for example `0x2876` and `treasury` at `0x207b068`. Read together, the writer gives the field of each key. Each field below was then checked against code of the game's own that reads it.

- **Numbers**: the game keeps its numbers as i32 thousandths (the ledger's getter for the number of cities, `0x6e01b0`, multiplies a count by 1000 to make one). In the writer:
  - `prestige` (token `0x29a1`) at `+0xcf8`, `stability` (`0x2886`) at `+0xbd8`, `treasury` (`0x2876`) at `+0xbb0`: `0x2df116`–`0x2df177`, each `lea rdx, [r15+field]`, the formatter `0x956f0` (`mov eax, [rdx]`, four bytes), then the token.
  - `manpower` (`0x283f`) at `+0xdb8` and `max_manpower` (`0x2973`) at `+0xdbc`: `0x2e00a9`–`0x2e00e7`. Manpower is in thousands of men, so its thousandths are men.
  - The ledger's table of getters (`0x6a7af0`, a name and a `std::function` each) reads the same fields: `treasury` `0x6e02c0` (`mov eax, [r8+0xbb0]`), `stability` `0x6e02f0` (`+0xbd8`), `prestige` `0x6e05a0` (`+0xcf8`), `manpower` `0x6e0540` (`+0xdb8`), `max_manpower` `0x6e0490` (`+0xdbc`).
- **Monarch points**: three i32 at `+0x260`, written as `powers` (`0x2ab2`) by the int-array writer `0x191900` (three values, `lea rdi, [r8+0xc]`) at `0x2e0b23`. The order is administrative, diplomatic, military: the tooltips of the triggers on each kind read `+0x260` (`0xcb41b5`, the function that names `HAVE_MORE_THAN_ADM_POWER`), `+0x264` (`0xcb4c23`, `HAVE_MORE_THAN_DIP_POWER`) and `+0x268` (`0xcb5683`, `HAVE_MORE_THAN_MIL_POWER`). The `adm_power` trigger itself (`0xcb3d70`, slot 6 of its vtable `0x1d04c60`) compares `+0x260` with its value. They are whole numbers, not thousandths: `0xab44a` multiplies one by 1000 to make a fixed-point number.
- **Overlord**: a tag at `+0x18f0`, written as `overlord` (`0x2cb6`) only when it is valid (`0x2ddee6`, `0x985c0` tests the valid byte).
- **Allies**: a list of the game's kind (`objects::list`) at `+0x19b8` whose items are the allies' tags themselves, written as `allies` (`0x2e9b`) by `0x4210e0`, which walks the list from its head (`+0`) through each node's next (`+0x10`), reading the tag in the node (`+0`), and skips the list when its count (`+0x10`) is 0. The AI walks it the same way at `0x3a37a7`; dozens of other functions read it, so it is the game's live state, not a copy made for saves.
- **Subjects**: a vector of tags (begin `+0x1a18`, end `+0x1a20`), written as `subjects` (`0x2edb`) by `0x420fc0`, which walks a vector of 8-byte tags. Clearing a country's diplomacy (`0x2e2ca7`) sets its end to its begin.
- **Subject types** (found 2026-10-30): which kind of subject a subject is (a vassal, a personal union, a tributary, an appanage) is not in the country: it is the subject relation's, a `dependency` in saves. The kinds are defined by `common/subject_types` (26 in the unmodded game), and a mod can add more, so the framework names one by its key there.
  - **The getter** `0x3d79f0`(country) answers the subject type the country is to its overlord, or null; a trigger's evaluation (`0xcfc4d0`, whose tooltip names `IS_SUBJECT_TYPE` at `0xcfcac2`, presumably `is_subject_of_type`'s) compares its result with the trigger's type, and the game walks an overlord's subjects (`+0x1a18`) calling it on each (`0x4e5ea4`–`0x4e5eea`). With no valid overlord (the tag's valid byte, `+0x18f7`) it answers null. Otherwise it takes the country's record of its overlord, in the table of relations at `[+0x1510]` (0x48 bytes by the other's index, `lea r8, [rax+rax*8]`); the record's first pointer leads to a country's diplomacy object, and its `+0x10` is the other's tag. In that diplomacy object, an array of the subject relations it takes part in: data at `+0x128`, 16-byte items, each the other country's index (i32) and the relation at `+8`, the count (i32) at `+0x134`. The item whose index is the overlord's holds the relation, and the relation's `+0x40` is the subject type.
  - **Where that diplomacy object is**: a country's diplomacy is at `+0x14f8` of the country (`countrydiplomacy.cpp`): adding a subject relation (`0xa5ff20`) hands `country + 0x14f8` of each side, with the other's tag, to `0x28bd00`, which looks for the other's index in the same array (`0x28be8c`, `+0x128`, `+0x134`) before adding to it (`+0x120`, an array of the kind the country manager holds) and marks the record of both (`[+0x18]` of the diplomacy object is the table at `+0x1510`). The framework follows the getter's own path and does not rely on this.
  - **A subject relation** is of class `0x1c81758` and 0x90 bytes; the game makes one when it reads a `dependency` (token `0x35c2`) at `0x4e5ced`, setting its class and its subject type (`+0x40`, `0x4e5d4b`). Its base, which every diplomatic relation shares, writes `first` (the overlord, `+0x10`), `second` (the subject, `+0x18`) and its dates (`0xa5c020`); its own writer (`0xa62b40`, its vtable's `+0x10`) writes `subject_type` (token `0x352a`) from the subject type's key, a `std::string` at `+0x10` of the subject type (`0xa62b6c`).
  - **Tested** (prototype-log.md, 2026-10-30): every subject relation in a 1470 save, 84 of six kinds, read through `country.get`; three of the kinds checked against the diplomacy page's tooltips.
- **Rivals**: a chain of nodes from `+0x2248`, each one a persistent object of its own (written as `rival`, `0x2cf7`, through its vtable): the rival's tag at `+0x10`, the next node at `+0x28`. The `is_rival` trigger (`0xc84590`, slot 6 of its vtable `0x1cf7d10`) walks it (`0xc848d6`) comparing the tags' indices with its target's.
- Lists written beside these, which the framework does not read: `extended_allies` `+0x19d0`, `guarantees` `+0x1a00`, `support_independence` `+0x19a0`, `coalition_friends` `+0x19e8`, `warnings` `+0x1b70`, `current_at_war_with` `+0x1b88`, `current_war_allies` `+0x1bc0`, `trade_embargoed_by` `+0x1bd8` (lists); `call_to_arms_friends` `+0x1ad8`, `condottieri_client` `+0x1a30` (vectors); `enemy` (`0x2aea`) entries from `+0x2218`.
- **Tested** (prototype-log.md, 2026-10-29, remote control C2): Castile's treasury, manpower and its maximum, stability, prestige and monarch points as the top bar shows them, twice two months apart; its allies, subjects (personal unions and vassals both) and rival as its diplomacy page shows them; the Ottomans' stability, prestige, manpower, ally and rivals as their diplomacy panel shows them; an overlord on two of Castile's subjects.
- **The `alliance_with` trigger** (`0xca0d60`) goes through another table: the country's relations to every other country at `[+0x1510]`, 0x48 bytes each, indexed by the other's index (`lea rdx, [rax+rax*8]`), whose first pointer leads back to the country and whose `+0x10` is the other's tag. The framework reads it only on the subject type getter's path (above).

## Wars

- **The wars going on** are pointers in an array on the world (`[0x233fe78]`): data at `+0x1e30`, count (i32) at `+0x1e3c`. The save writer (`0x74ff30`) writes each as `active_war` (token `0x28f7`, `0x751883`), and the wars that ended, a list from `+0x1e40`, as `previous_war`. `0x291997` walks both, `0x75f540` finds a war by its name.
- **A war** is of class `0x1d1f768`, set by its constructors `0xd856b0` and `0xd859b0`. Its writer (`0xd88520`, slot 2 of the vtable) writes its name, a `std::string` at `+0x28` (the constructor sets its size `+0x38` to 0 and its capacity `+0x40` to 15), its participants (`+0xc0`, 0x90 bytes each), then `attackers` (`0x28f5`) and `defenders` (`0x28f6`), vectors of tags at `+0x48` and `+0x78`, with `0x420fc0`. `0xd894f0` compares a tag with the first attacker and the first defender before anything else: the first of each side is its leader. `+0x148` to `+0x158` hold three more tags (presumably the original attacker and defender), which the framework does not read. Tested: a war's name and both sides as the war panel shows them, the defenders' leader starred there; the panel lists the attackers after their leader in another order than the vector's.

What the framework relies on is in `objects::campaign::{country, country_diplomacy, subject_relation, subject_type, war}`, `world::WARS`, `classes::WAR` and `classes::SUBJECT_RELATION`, and the dependency points named "country …", "… points", "subject type", "subject relation", "subject type key", "wars", "war" and "war leaders".
