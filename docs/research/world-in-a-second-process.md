# The world in a second process (W2, W3)

Whether a second process could hold the simulation's world at the same addresses as the process that runs it, so that the interface and the drawing read a copy of the world held still while the simulation runs on: phase 3 of [drawing-thread.md](../plans/drawing-thread.md), parts W2 (the game's allocations taken over) and W3 (a second process with the world at the same addresses), and W4's handover in so far as its cost is Windows'. W1, the seams between the world and the interface, is a note of its own. Baseline 1.37.5; addresses are RVAs. Read from `eu4.exe` and measured on 2026-11-10 ([the run](../prototype-log/2026-11-10-world-in-a-second-process.md)).

## Where the game's memory comes from

- **One heap for everything.** The game's `operator new` (`0x1a332d4`) calls the C runtime's `malloc` (`0x1a4963c` → `0x1a6837c`), which is `HeapAlloc([0x233d850], 0, size)` (`0x1a683ba`); `0x233d850` is set from `GetProcessHeap()` at `0x1a72e98`. The C runtime is linked into `eu4.exe`: the executable imports no `ucrtbase` or `vcruntime`. Its `free` (`0x1a6833c`), `calloc` (`0x1a6989c`), `realloc` (`0x1a6dd98`) and `_msize` (`0x1a706f4`) use the same handle; nothing else in `eu4.exe` calls `HeapAlloc`, `HeapReAlloc` or `HeapCreate`. So the world, the databases loaded at startup, the interface's windows and the graphics' wrappers are all blocks of the process's default heap, made by the same function on the same thread.
- **The others in the same heap.** `tbb.dll` allocates through `ucrtbase` (it imports `api-ms-win-crt-heap-l1-1-0`), which is the same process heap by another path: the game calls TBB's allocator at 31 places (`?allocate_via_handler_v3`, from `0x7dbd5` on) and its cache-aligned one at 7 (`?NFS_Allocate`). Direct3D's runtime and the driver keep most of theirs outside the heaps. The memory account listed six heaps at the main menu and after the 1780.7.1 save was loaded, the process's holding all but some 100 KB; the framework's own allocations are blocks of the process heap too.
- **The large blocks** are the heap's own. The game's only `VirtualAlloc` (`0x171f571`, in `0x171f530`) serves 24 objects of 0x48 bytes (`0x212cd0` and its neighbors, each in a page of its own that `VirtualProtect` makes read-only after it is filled, `0x212c18`), not the world's arrays. The two blocks of about 55 MB that M1 found outside the heap's regions, the countries' and the provinces' arrays, are therefore `HeapAlloc`'s large blocks, which the heap takes from `NtAllocateVirtualMemory` itself (_inferred_ from the absence of another allocator; the heap's list of its large blocks was not walked).
- **How much.** At the main menu, the game's heap held 1.80 GB (2.04 GB committed). With the 1780.7.1 save loaded: 2.08 GB (2.36 GB committed) of a process's 4.35 GB of private bytes, and 1.99 GB outside the heaps. So a late world adds some 0.3 GB to a heap already holding 1.8 GB of what the startup loaded, which the world's objects point into (their types, cultures, religions and the rest).

## The address space

- **The images are where they are in every process.** `eu4.exe` has `DYNAMIC_BASE` and `HIGH_ENTROPY_VA` (DllCharacteristics `0x8160`) and relocations, and its preferred base `0x140000000` is not used. Windows gives an image the same base in every process until the machine restarts: two test games at once had `eu4.exe` at `0x7ff655300000` and `ntdll`, `kernel32`, `KERNELBASE`, `ucrtbase`, `tbb.dll`, `steam_api64.dll`, `d3d9.dll`, `d3dx9_43.dll`, `nvd3dumx.dll`, `EOSSDK-Win64-Shipping.dll`, `pops_api.dll` and `nakama-cpp.dll` each at the same base in both. The framework's core, loaded from a path of each test game's own, was not. So the world's pointers into `eu4.exe` (vtables, constant data) and into the DLLs would hold in a second process; that Windows keeps an image's base so is its practice, not a promise, and would be checked at the second process's start.
- **The heaps are not.** The same two games: the process heap at `0x25979e80000` and at `0x1cb87640000`, the PEB at `0xadca40d000` and `0x4b072c3000`, 611 and 615 private allocations, none at the other's address. ASLR places them per process.
- **A range can be kept free in a process before its heap is made.** A process created suspended has its image, `ntdll`, its PEB and its first thread's stack, but not yet its heap, which `ntdll` makes as the first thread starts. Tried with a throwaway child (`cmd.exe`): a placeholder of 64 GB reserved in it at `0x100000000000` (`VirtualAlloc2`, `MEM_RESERVE | MEM_RESERVE_PLACEHOLDER`) before it was resumed stayed reserved, and its heap went to `0x203ad550000`. In the game's own process the earliest code of the framework's is the proxy's `DllMain` (`version.dll` is an import of `eu4.exe`, so it runs before the executable's entry and its C runtime's start, and the core's bootstrap installs every dependency point there); by then the process heap exists, at a place of the system's choosing, but no block of the game's has been allocated.
- **A section can be put at a chosen address in another process.** A placeholder of 64 MB in a suspended child replaced by a view of a section backed by the paging file (`MapViewOfFile3`, `MEM_REPLACE_PLACEHOLDER`) was at `0x100000000000` in the child, and what the parent wrote through its own view the child read, and read again as it changed.

## What the world in a second process would take

The simulation's process (A) and the interface's (B), each an `eu4.exe` with the framework:

1. **A's game allocations in one range, B's in another.** The game's C runtime's five functions, and TBB's two allocators through `eu4.exe`'s imports, replaced from the proxy's `DllMain` on by an allocator of the framework's over a range reserved at a fixed address: in A over memory allocated with `MEM_WRITE_WATCH`, in B elsewhere. Direct3D, the driver and Windows keep their own heaps; B keeps A's range free from its start (created suspended, by A or a launcher). B never allocates in A's range and frees nothing of it: a block of A's B lets go is left, and B's `realloc` of one makes a new block in its own range. The allocators' records lie in their own ranges, so copying A's range copies its records too, which B never reads: the heap's records are no obstacle. What can be done: Windows offers all of it. What is not known: whether everything the world's objects point to is in the game's heap; TBB's containers and anything the C runtime's `_aligned_malloc` or Windows allocates on the game's behalf were not looked for.
2. **A handover.** At a day's end, A finds the pages it wrote (`GetWriteWatch` with reset) and copies them into a section B maps at A's addresses, while B is between two updates. Measured with 2 GB watched and 17,920 pages of 4 KB written at random: 17,602 pages, 69 MB, found in 6 to 7 ms; copied into a section on one thread in 14 ms once the section's pages were committed, 42 to 45 ms the first time. So an ordinary day's 40 to 70 MB (M1) would stop B for some 20 ms on one thread, a month's turn's 167 MB some 50 ms, less on several threads. B's own writes into A's range (the views of section pages B writes are its own copies until the next handover) are lost at each handover, unless they are kept apart and written again (W1).
3. **The globals.** `eu4.exe`'s `.data` is 4.9 MB of world, databases, the interface and the graphics together; the 0.2 MB a day writes would be copied global by global, from a list of which belong to the world (W1). B's interface would read the world and the databases through them; the databases B loaded itself would go unused, and B would need A's, since the world's objects point at A's and the game compares such pointers.
4. **Commands back to A.** The game writes every command it posts as a block and reads it back (the local handler, `0x158053d`, `0x158097a`; [commands.md](commands.md)), as for a multiplayer game. B's player's commands could go to A in that form.
5. **B's start and its session.** A second game reaches the main menu in 18 to 36 s, as a test game does, and holds its own 1.8 GB of the startup's data and its own Direct3D device and textures, some 4 GB of private bytes in all beside A's. Its in-game interface (the in-game idler, the map manager, the session's windows) is made by the session's start, which in the game is a new game's or a load's: the code that makes the world. A way for B to make the interface over a world it did not make was not looked for, and none is known.

## What stands in the way

- **The world and the interface share pages.** Copying is by page, and the interface's objects and the world's lie side by side in the same heap's pages, made by the same `operator new` on the same thread. Unless every allocation is told apart as the world's or the interface's when it is made, A's pages carry A's interface objects, and B's objects cannot be in A's range at all. Telling them apart is not in the game's code: no thread, phase or call site separates them in general (a container's growth is one function for both). Shown only as far as the one `operator new` and one heap go; a separation by the allocating code's address (`eu4\source\graphics\` is `0xde6000`–`0x1415000`) was not tried.
- **B has no way into a session.** As above: the game makes the in-game interface only on the way that makes the world.
- **B's writes into the world.** The interface writes into the world's objects (a unit's model at its place's `+0x70`, the texts floating on a province at `+0x2d0`, caches); in B those writes are to A's pages and are undone at every handover unless they are known and written again (W1).
- **The globals**, mixed in one section, which W1 would have to list.

## Not verified

- That the 55 MB blocks are the heap's large blocks (the heap was not walked).
- That nothing the world points to lies outside the game's heap (TBB's, `_aligned_malloc`'s, Windows').
- The cost of B's soft faults on a view remapped at every handover; the copy on several threads.
- That image bases stay the same across processes in every case (two games, one boot, one machine).
- Anything of B in the game: no second process was started against a first.

## Verdict

What Windows has to give is there and is cheap: the images at the same addresses, a range kept free in a process before its heap exists, sections at chosen addresses, write watching that finds a day's pages in milliseconds. What the game would have to give is not: one heap holds the world, the databases and the interface without anything to tell them apart, and only the code that makes the world makes the in-game interface. Phase 3 so needs the game's every allocation sorted at the moment it is made, a way into a session that does not exist, and the seams of W1 kept by hand across every handover. It is research with no known way through, not a part to build.
