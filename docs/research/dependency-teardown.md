# A subject relation's end, and the crash in it

Baseline 1.37.5. All addresses are RVAs. Found on 2026-11-06 from a player's crash (an observer game with mods, 1478, crashed in `0xa5e14f`), its minidump, the three half-yearly saves before it, and reading eu4.exe with a Capstone script; the defect and its repair were then tried in the running game ([A subject relation's end](../prototype-log/2026-11-06-subject-relation-end.md), 2026-11-06).

## What crashed

- The game was executing a peace offer (`0x1c7b840`) between Khorasan and the Timurids, through the diplomatic action command's execute `0x4e7c50` ([diplomacy.md](diplomacy.md)). The treaty's step `0x9910e0`, called by the treaty's execution `0x976730` after its terms (`0x9786c7`), goes on through `0x367390` (country), `0xa020f0` and `0xa00270` (province), `0x3663b0` and `0x364f10` (country) to `0xa61900`. The Timurids had 5 provinces in the last save; presumably the peace took the last of them and the game was ending their relations.
- `0xa61900`(dependency, …, `r8b`) ends a subject relation (`dependency` in saves): here the Timurids (`+0x10`, the overlord) over Transoxiana (`+0x18`, the subject). Read from its strings and calls, it is the relation's end in `relation.cpp`; the forum's macOS stack traces of the same crash name it `CDependency::OnDisable`, reached from `CCountry::Annex` and `CCountry::ClearRelations`.
- With `r8b` set it walks the subject's `support_independence` list (`+0x19a0`, below), and for each country in it looks for that country's support relation with the subject: the country's record of the subject in the table at `[+0x1510]` (0x48 bytes by index), the record's first pointer to a diplomacy object, its array of support relations at `+0x170` (16-byte items, the other country's index and the relation at `+8`, the count at `+0x17c`), searched for the record's `+0x10` tag. Found, it ends that relation (`0xa5e130`) and deletes it (`0xa5bb00`, `0x40` bytes). Not found, the compiled code still calls `0xa5e130` with null (`0xa619e5`: `xor ecx, ecx; call`). `0xa5e130` hands its relation to `CDiplomacy::RemActive` (`0x4e1570` on `[0x233fe78] + 0x1bb8`), which logs `[diplomacy.cpp:225]: !pRelation CDiplomacy::RemActive` and returns, and then reads `[null + 0x10]`.
- The country in the list was the Nogai (index `0xf6`, `r15` at the crash). No save has a support relation between the Nogai and Transoxiana; the Nogai are Transoxiana's neighbor.

## The list it walks

- **The `support_independence` list** (`+0x19a0`, token `0x2f2f`; read at `0x2def9f`, written at `0x2d6ce3`, cleared with the country's diplomacy at `0x2e2c9b`) is a cache. `0x93fe30`(country, source label), the country's refresh of its diplomatic caches, clears it with the country's other lists (`0x9400a3`; allies `+0x19b8`, `+0x19d0`, `+0x19e8` and more) and builds them again; a country takes into `+0x19a0` (`0x9419d3`) each other country found in its own array of support relations (`+0x14f8 + 0x170` = `+0x1668`, count `+0x1674`) while it has a valid overlord (`+0x18f0`, the valid byte `+0x18f7`).
- So the list holds every country the subject shares a support relation with, both those supporting its independence and those whose independence it supports: in the 1478.1.1 save, Transoxiana (the Timurids' vassal) lists `MUL` for the relation `first="TRS" second="MUL"`, and the Kazakhs list both `SHY` (`first="SHY" second="KHA"`) and `CHG` (`first="KHA" second="CHG"`).
- **A list of the game's kind** (`objects::list`): head `+0`, tail `+8`, count `+0x10`; each node `0x20` bytes from `operator new` (`0x1a332d4`): the item at `+0`, the previous node at `+8`, the next at `+0x10`. Appending is `0xdf120`; clearing (`0xd0020`) deletes every node (`0x17394f0`, size `0x20`) and zeroes head, tail and count.

## The defect

- Inside the walk, after each relation it ends, `0xa61900` refreshes both countries' caches: `0x93fe30`(the other country, `relation.cpp:1247`) at `0xa61a05` and `0x93fe30`(the subject, `relation.cpp:1248`) at `0xa61a2f`. The subject's refresh clears the very list being walked, deleting the node the walk stands on, and the walk then takes that freed node's next (`0xa61ace`: `mov rax, [rbx+0x10]`).
- What it finds there depends on the heap. While the freed node lies untouched, its old next leads on through the old (freed) nodes and nothing shows. Once the refresh has given the node again to a list it builds, the walk goes on into that other list. The refresh builds a dozen lists of `0x20`-byte nodes for each of the two countries, among them, through `0x40e770` (its only caller is the refresh, at `0x940fa5` and `0x941049`), `home_neighbours` (`+0x1aa8`, token `0x2e93`) and `core_neighbours` (`+0x1ac0`, `0x2e94`), appended with `0xdf120` at `0x40ea5f` and `0x40ea05`; `neighbours` (`+0x1a90`, `0x2e92`) is a vector. Transoxiana's two lists were `KHO SHY NOG TIM CHG` in the last save, the Nogai among them. A country the walk reaches so that shares no support relation with the subject gives the null above, and the game crashes; one with such a relation would see it ended though it was never in the list.
- It needs only a subject with one entry in its list whose subject relation ends while the refresh runs: an overlord annexed or destroyed, independence, release. Transoxiana had a single entry (`MUL`) in the last save: its node, freed after the first relation ended, could only lead on if it was taken again, since its own next was null.
- **Tried** (2026-11-06): in that save played as the Nogai, `annex TIM` in the game console ends the Timurids' rule over Transoxiana through this walk, over Transoxiana's list (`MUL`). With the heap census's quarantine, which keeps what the walk frees and fills it with `0xdd`, the game's own walk faults every time, before it ends.
- That the list mixes both directions is a second oddity: ending the Timurids' rule over Transoxiana also ends Transoxiana's support of Multan's independence. Not looked into further.

## The repair

`core/src/repairs/supporters.rs`. The refresh `0x93fe30` is replaced; the walk's start (`0xa6196d`, past the check that the walk is asked for) and its end (`0xa61b15`, reached only from the walk, which `0xa61ad8` leaves when the next node is null) jump to stubs. While a walk goes on, a refresh of its subject on its thread waits, and the walk's end does it once, with the label of the last one that waited: the list stays whole under the walk, and the subject's caches end as the last refresh in the game's walk leaves them. The other country's refresh in the walk (`0xa61a05`) and every other refresh stay the game's. Tried: the same annexation passed, its walk logged, and 60 days on the quarantine had found no block written to.

## Not established

- Which list took the freed node in the player's crash: the minidump keeps no country memory. The Nogai were in two of the lists the same refresh builds (above), and in none of Transoxiana's others in the save.
- Whether `0x93fe30` ever returns early here (it does while `[0x233fe78] + 0x7485` is set, when `0x7495e0` says no, or when `[0x233fea0] + 0x40` is set); in the crash and in the runs it cleared the list.
- Whether, between the walk's refreshes the repair puts off, the game reads the subject's caches and decides otherwise for them being as they were before the walk (`0x442fe0` and `0x93e630` on the subject in each step were not followed).
- Whether the other callers of `0xa61900` (`0x3d7f40`, `0x3d7f62`, `0x513b80`, `0x513d82`) pass `r8b` set and so walk the list too.
- Of `0xa5e130`'s eleven direct callers, `0xa619e7` is the only one that may pass null; the others test it first (`test`, `je`) or pass a relation just found.
