# Diplomacy (for `game::diplomacy`)

Baseline 1.37.5. All addresses are RVAs. Found by reading eu4.exe on 2026-10-29; what was checked in the running game is marked "tested" (prototype-log.md, 2026-10-29, remote control C8). The session that takes the player's commands is in `objects::session`; an offer another country makes to the player, which the player answers, is C7's (GLOSSARY "Offer").

## Diplomatic actions

- **Every diplomatic action** derives from a base whose vtable is `0x1c3e578` (its abstract methods are `_purecall`, `0x1a35124`). Fields (diplomaticaction.cpp; the constructors set them, such as the declaration of war's at `0x500492`): `+8` the persistent object type `0x165`; `+0x10` the country making it and `+0x18` again (the actor), `+0x20` the country it is made to (tags); `+0x28` and `+0x2c` the day it was made and sent; `+0x30` an AI plan's id, -1 for none; `+0x34` its state, 0 a proposal, 1 declined, 2 accepted; `+0x38` a byte, 1 for an action that starts something (an alliance, a war), 0 for one that ends it (dissolving an alliance); `+0x3c` the id it gets on reaching a human player (C7).
- **Its methods**, by vtable offset: `+0` the destructor (action, flags; flag 1 frees it); `+0x40` makes the command that sends it (`0x4e9930`, shared by every kind: copies the action through `+0xb0`, sets the copy's `+0x2c` to the day, and makes a `0x58`-byte command of class `0x1c814b0` holding the copy at `+0x50`); `+0x48` carries it out once accepted; `+0x80`, `+0x88` and `+0x90` the checks below; `+0x98` its type, the token of its key in saves (`0x293b` declarewar, `0x293c` requestpeace, `0x293d` allianceaction); `+0xb0` copies it; `+0xb8` whether the AI accepts it; `+0xd0` whether it needs an answer; `+0xf0` whether it is accepted without asking.
- **The three kinds the framework sends**:
  - **Alliance** (`0x1c80720`, `0x40` bytes, the size its destructor `0x129450` frees): it has no constructor of its own; the game fills its fields in place, as `0xdb49f4` does on the stack before asking its check, and its copy `0x513060`. `+0x38` 1 offers an alliance, 0 dissolves one.
  - **Declaration of war** (`0x1c80d90`, `0x98` bytes): constructor `0x500480`(action, from, to, flag, day, war goal), which copies the war goal (`0xd9aaf0`) to `+0x48` and clears three vectors at `+0x50`, `+0x68` and `+0x80`; the flag goes to `+0x40` (0 as the diplomacy panel's and most of the AI's), `+0x42` is the window's "colonial war" button (0 at first). `+0x50` holds the allies called (`attacker` in a save, written at `0x50bae0`) and `+0x68` the allies promised land (`promised_land`, `0x50bb80`), each a `std::vector` of tags; an action as made calls no ally (below). The AI makes its own with the same constructor (`0x13eb9a`) and asks `+0x88` and `+0x80` before it sends it.
  - **Peace offer** (`0x1c7b840`, `0x210` bytes): constructor `0x598af0`(action, from, to, demand, treaty, day), which copies the treaty to `+0x48` (`0x1c0` bytes, up to the byte at `+0x208`); the demand flag at `+0x41` is the peace view's tab (`[view + 0x660]`). A treaty is made by `0x975d40`(treaty, actor, other, demand) with no terms, a white peace, and destroyed by `0x9761c0`; the peace view keeps a demand treaty (player, enemy, 1) and an offer treaty (enemy, player, 0), and the war panel's "no peace" check (`0x148b710`) makes a demand with no terms to ask the action whether a peace is possible at all.

## The checks, and the game's reasons

- **`+0x80`** is the action's own conditions (alliance `0x511d60`, declaration `0x508db0`, peace `0x59b450`), asked with a null `std::string` by the command as the game carries it out. Given one, each failed condition is appended by `0x14bb940`, which writes `§R- `, the localized text and `§!` and a line break. The peace offer's looks for the war between the two countries in the maker's relations table (`[country + 0x1510]`, `0x48` bytes per other country, whose first pointer has the wars at `+0x50`, count `+0x5c`) and answers false, with no reason, when there is none.
- **`+0x88`** is what the window that sends the action asks, for its agree button (`0xee4685` in the declare war window: `+0x88(action, 0, 0)`): the base `0x4e9d00` (both countries exist; favors; the wait after sending a diplomat to the same country, "ENROUTE"; a free diplomat, "NODIPLOMATS"; then `+0x80`), and each kind's own on top (alliance `0x512c70`, declaration `0x50a0a0`: stability, truces, the first month of the game and of the last war; peace `0x59b630`). Given a string, each then appends the action's description after a line break: `OFFERALLIANCEDESC` (`0x512cc3`), `WARDESC` (`0x50a706`), `DECWARDESC` (`0x59b86d`).
- **`+0x90`** is what the diplomacy panel asks to enable an action's button (`0xef30d1`), and its tooltip is the same with a string (`0xef2276`): the alliance's `0x5129d0` refuses a second alliance offer on the same day ("MUST_WAIT_UNTIL_NEXT_ALLIANCE_OFFER": the world's record below, or the maker's `+0x2494`, the day its last alliance offer was carried out) and adds `+0x88`; the peace offer's `0x59b8d0` refuses a second peace offer on the same day (the world's record, or the maker's `+0x24a0`) and lets the player open the peace view otherwise; the declaration's `0x509e30` asks `0x509ef0` of the maker and appends `WARDESC`. A third argument, nonzero, skips the diplomat checks; the panel and the windows pass 0.
- **What the framework asks**: `+0x90` and `+0x88`, with no string, as the panel and the window do before the player can send; when either says no, both again with a string, and the refusal is their lines as plain Unicode (`game_text::plain`), each once, without the bullet, and without the description, which the framework localizes with `0x14b34a0`(out, key, 0, 0) from the key the check uses. Tested: alliance refusals for opinion (two reasons at once), for a second offer on one day; a declaration refused for the first month of the game, then for three reasons at once (a diplomat recently sent, already at war, a war begun less than a month ago); peace refused for the diplomat's wait, for a second offer on one day, and for a subject; the descriptions never showed.

## Sending one, as the player

- **The diplomacy panel** lists the actions as entries (`diploactionentry`, constructor `0xef1db0`, vtable `0x1d3b810`) each holding a copy of an action at `+0x140`; a click (`0xef21c0` → `0xf0acd0`) opens, by the action's type, the declare war window (`0xee41f0`, `0x378` bytes), the peace view (the map manager's method `+0x260`), or for an alliance the default diplomacy window (`0xedef60`, "DefaultDiplomacyDialog").
- **The window's agree** (`0xedfa40`, the default window's vtable `0x1d3c180` `+0x50`): for an alliance (and three other kinds) it writes the world's record of the last alliance offer, `[world + 0x1d94]` the maker's tag and `[world + 0x1d90]` the day; for a peace offer `+0x1da0` and `+0x1d9c`; sets the action's `+0x28` to the day; has the action make its command (`+0x40`) and posts it to the session it gets from the map manager (`[map manager]+0x80`) with `0x14ca210`, the post that takes a `std::unique_ptr`. The declare war window's agree (`0xee7780`) first asks `0x507200` whether the declaration costs something (no casus belli, a truce broken) and, if so, opens a confirmation before the same send. The framework posts with `0x14ca2d0`, the same post taking the command itself, and frees a command the session does not take with its destructor (`0x4e7470`), where `0x14ca210` would leak it.

## The war goals offered

- **The declare war window** (`0xee6ac0`) asks `0xd9eb40`(from, to, list, filter) for them: a list of class `0x1c3d1e0` (`0x18` bytes; the war goals at `+8`, count `+0x14`, i32) that owns what it holds and frees it in its destructor (`0x114b80` → `0x116050`), and an empty `std::function` as the filter. It walks the casus belli the maker holds against the other in their relations table (`0x28d560`) and those it has by its triggers (`0xd9ee50`), and for each type adds its war goals (`0xd9d220`); a casus belli type with `exclusive = yes` (the byte at `+0x159`) clears what was there and ends the walk.
- **A war goal** (`0x60` bytes, base vtable `0x1d20358`, a subclass per kind of war goal type): `+0x10` its type, whose key is the `std::string` at `+0x18` (`take_core`); `+0x18` the province it is about (i32, -1 none); `+0x1c` the country it is about (a tag, valid when `+0x23` is nonzero); `+0x40` its casus belli's type, whose key is at `+0x18` (`cb_core`); as its save writer `0xd9bea0` writes them. Its method `+0x40`(war goal, out `std::string`) writes its name as the window shows it (`0xee391a`).
- **The window** groups them by casus belli and picks the first; when there is none it picks the war goal at `[0x2340030]`, declaring war without a casus belli, and it hides that choice when the first one's casus belli is exclusive. The framework lists them in the list's order, that one last when the window would offer it, and declares war for the first that has what the client gave.
- **Tested**: as the Ottomans in 1444, against Byzantium and six other neighbors only `none` (`nowargoal`, "无战争目标"); against Albania `cb_core`, `take_core`, for Lezhe (4175) and Kruje (4750), named "收回核心省份莱什" and "收回核心省份克鲁耶", then `none`. Not compared with the window on screen.

## Calling allies to arms

- **Who gets a call to arms**: as the game carries out a declaration a human player made, it sends a call to arms only to the allies in `+0x50` (`0x506851` walks that vector; `0x502e80` sends the calls). Tested: with `+0x50` left empty, allied Dulkadir did not join the war on Albania (the first run); with it filled as below, each ally put there joined and none other did.
- **What the declare war window gathers** (`0xee633b`: `0x503e70`(action, out, 1)): a `0x38`-byte result holding the countries on the declarer's side (a `std::vector` at `+0`) and on the other side (`+0x18`), the declarer and the country declared on first; each item is `0x18` bytes: the country (a tag) at `+0`, why it is there (i32) at `+8`, through which country (a tag, unaligned) at `+0xc`, and a byte at `+0x14`, nonzero when it is on that side without being called (the two countries themselves, and those that join with another, such as subjects). `0x124150` frees a vector of them.
- **Its allies list** (`0xee6550`) has an entry (`0x320` bytes, made at `0xedfcd8`) for each country on the declarer's side but the declarer and the country declared on: `+0x50` the window, `+0x60` a copy of the side's item, `+0x78` 1 in the allies list (0 in the enemies list), `+0x79` the promise land box, 1 as made.
- **The call box** (`0xee32e7`) asks the check `0xee03f0`(entry, action, whether the ally is not among those promised land, reasons) and, when it allows the call, puts the ally at the end of `+0x50` (`0xee3405`, growing the vector with `0xdfde0` when it is full); the window gathers again after a call.
- **The check** reads the entry's country, its tag's valid byte, whether it joins anyway (`+0x74`; such an ally has no box to tick), whether it is in the allies list, through whom and why it is there; whether a human player plays the country (`[world + 0x1d48]`, a `std::vector` of i32 by country index, nonzero for a human player's); and the window's list of enemies (the window's element at `+8` finds its list box by name, vtable `+0x1a8`, whose entries, `+0x58`, are a singly linked list, next at `+0x10`, none when the first is null), refusing an ally the other side lists too (`ALLIED_TO_ENEMY`, the key at `0x1d1f5e0`). For an AI ally it answers whether the AI would accept the call, and with a `std::string` writes what the box's tooltip shows: the verdict, the sums for and against, and each factor. Tested: Candar, called against Albania, "估计他们不会接受。", for 55 and against -1107, from trust +50, the Ottomans' reputation +5, "詹达尔没有欠奥斯曼至少10点人情：-1000" (not owing the Ottomans 10 favors) and "距离遥远的战争：-107". The run then gave the Ottomans favors with each ally through the game console's `favors` before declaring again.
- **What the framework does**: it gathers as the window does, asks the check for each ally from the top with an entry and a window of its own (the window's list box of any name has no entries, so the framework stands for the enemies list itself: an ally on the other side that joins it anyway, or that the check would let the other side call while the AI plays it, is refused as the window refuses it), puts each ally the check allows into `+0x50` as the call box does, and gathers again after each. `+0x68` stays empty, as when the promise land boxes are left alone.
- **Tested** (C8's run after review): as the Ottomans with Dulkadir, Candar and Georgia allied, a declaration on Karaman calling Dulkadir alone had Dulkadir join; Candar, Karaman's rival, and Georgia stayed out and stayed allies. One on Trebizond calling every ally the window allows called Candar and Dulkadir, who joined; Georgia, Trebizond's ally too, was passed over, joined the defenders and was no longer the Ottomans' ally. One on Byzantium calling none had no ally join.

## Carrying one out, and the answer

- **The command's execution** (`0x4e7c50`, its vtable's `+0x48`, also called directly for actions the game sends itself): asks `+0x80`; for a proposal (state 0) it records the day on the maker (`+0x2494` alliance, `+0x24a0` peace); then, when the action needs an answer (`+0xd0`) and the other country is the AI's (its entry in the array at `[world + 0x1d48]` is 0, presumably no human player, and `0x2cd570` finds its AI), copies it, sets the copy's state to 2 when it is accepted without asking or to 1 + whether the AI accepts (`0x4e81c1`), and posts the copy's command (`0xbfe50`); a human player's gets a copy in its list at `+0x18d8` (C7). `0x4ea820` then resolves the action; it calls `+0x48`, which presumably carries out an accepted proposal, and a declaration of war (which needs no answer) is carried out there at once (tested: the war had begun at the next safe point).
- **What the framework sees**: an observer of `0x4e7c50` keeps, for each command whose action is one of the three kinds and made by the player, its kind, the country it is made to and its state. An action sent is answered by what is kept after it: the other country's answer (state 1 or 2) for a proposal; for a declaration, the declaration carried out (state 0) and a war with that country on the other side in the player's country snapshot.
- **Tested**: the proposal was carried out at the frame after it was posted and the answer at the one after that, paused and running: remote requests answered 43 to 136 ms after they ran, the probe's alliance 3 frames after it was sent. Karaman declined an alliance; Dulkadir accepted one (and showed as an ally in `country.get`); Venice declined a white peace in the war, then accepted a separate one after the game console's `winwars` gave the player the war's score, and left it with its subject Naxos; Albania declined a white peace. The declaration on Albania for Kruje began "奥斯曼收复克鲁耶之战", with Albania, Venice and Naxos as defenders.

## Not established

- `+0x80` of a declaration, the third vector its constructor clears, which `0x503e70` reads among others.
- Whether an ally the check lets the player call can still decline the call: each one called in the run joined.
- How the AI calls its own allies to arms, and promising land (`+0x68`), which the framework leaves alone.
- `+0x40` of a declaration, which some of the AI's calls set to 1.
- A human player as the other country (multiplayer, out of scope), whose answer comes later through C7's offers.
- The costs the declare war window confirms before sending (`0x507200`, `0x506e60`, `0x50bed0`); the framework sends without that confirmation, as a player who confirms does.
