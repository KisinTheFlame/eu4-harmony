# War and peace (for `game::peace`, `game::occupation`, `game::wars` and the call to arms)

Baseline 1.37.5. All addresses are RVAs. Found by reading eu4.exe on 2026-11-05 with a Capstone script over the PE file and the token table (`0x1fd8660`, [architecture.md](architecture.md)); what was checked in the running game is in [prototype-log.md](../prototype-log.md), 2026-11-05, player actions A5. The diplomatic actions, their checks and how one is sent are in [diplomacy.md](diplomacy.md); this file adds the peace treaty's terms, the AI's answer with its factors, calls to arms in a war going on, the transfer of occupation and the events of A5.

## The peace treaty and its terms

- **A treaty** (`0x1c0` bytes, vtable `0x1c578b8`; constructor `0x975d40`(treaty, actor, other, demand), destructor `0x9761c0`, copy `0x975f40`): `+0x10` the other, `+0x18` the actor (tags), `+0x20` the demand flag; `+0x88` a `std::vector` of pointers to every term it holds; `+0xd0` 40 vectors, one per kind of term (`0x18` bytes each), of those chosen; `+0xf0` the war score cost of what is chosen, in thousandths. The constructor fills `+0x88` with every term the peace view offers (`0x97bea0`, `0x978cd0`, `0x97ed00` among the calls that do it; not followed one by one), none chosen. The peace view keeps two: the demand tab's at `[view + 0x48]` (player, enemy, 1) and the offer tab's at `[view + 0x208]` (enemy, player, 0), the tab at `[view + 0x660]`.
- **Its written form** (`0x981690`, the treaty's `+0x10`; reading one key `0x981170`): `giver`, `taker`, `givers`, `takers`, `war_givers`, `war_takers`, `is_demand`, `apply_penalties` when not 1, then each term under its type token as a block, with `index` (its place among its kind's chosen) when chosen. A peace offer (`requestpeace`, writer `0x59bd20`) writes `diplomats`, `demand` and its treaty under `terms`.
- **A term** ("peace option", 40 kinds; the factory `0x99ff90` makes one by kind, from a jump table at `0x9a0f98`): the base (`0x30` bytes; writer `0x99b550`) `+0x10` `offer_giver`, `+0x18` `offer_taker`, `+0x22` `giver` (tags), `+0x20` `is_selected`, the chosen flag; kinds that name another country add `taker` at `+0x30` (writer `0x99e610`); `po_demand_provinces` (writer `0x9a3960`), `po_revoke_cores` and `po_return_cores` (`0x99e7c0`) add a province pointer at `+0x38`, written as its `+0x20`, `province_id`; `po_gold` (`0x9b4440`) the amount at `+0x30` (thousandths of a ducat, `value`); `po_concede_colonial_region` (`0x9c9e20`) the region at `+0x30`, whose key is the `std::string` at `[region + 0x10] + 0x138`; a scripted peace treaty, kind 39 (`0xac9be0`), its type at `+0x30`, whose key is the `std::string` at `+0x10` (`type`).
- **The kinds, in the game's order** (kind: type token's name): 0 `po_gold`, 1 `po_become_vassal`, 2 `po_subjugate_vassal`, 3 `po_demand_provinces`, 4 `po_revoke_cores`, 5 `po_return_cores`, 6 `po_give_up_claim`, 7 `po_war_reparations`, 8 `po_trade_power`, 9 `po_humiliate_rival`, 10 `po_humiliate`, 11 `po_concede_defeat`, 12 `revoke_reform`, 13 `po_change_hre_religion`, 14 `po_annul_treaties`, 15 `po_release_annexed`, 16 `po_enforce_military_access`, 17 `po_enforce_fleet_basing_rights`, 18 `po_independence`, 19 `po_release_vassals`, 20 `po_transfer_vassals`, 21 `po_change_religion`, 22 `po_change_government`, 23 `po_dismantle_revolution`, 24 `po_revoke_elector`, 25 `po_enforce_rebel_demands`, 26 `po_form_personal_union`, 27 `po_concede_colonial_region`, 28 `po_end_rivalry`, 29 `po_take_mandate`, 30 `po_become_tributary_state`, 31 `po_subjugate_tributary_state`, 32 `po_steer_trade`, 33 `po_join_empire`, 34 `po_spread_revolution`, 35 `po_force_migration`, 36 `po_force_out_colonizers`, 37 `po_pillage_capital_state`, 38 `po_guaranteed_join_empire`, 39 `scriptable_diplomatic_action`. Those with a `taker`: 1–6, 14, 15, 19, 20, 28, 30, 31, 37 (by their writers).
- **A term's methods**, by vtable offset: `+0x78`(term, out) a pointer to its kind (i32) and, for a scripted one, its type; `+0xa0` its type token; `+0x100`(term, out `std::string`, treaty) its name as the peace view lists it (`0x1313230`); `+0x128`(term, treaty, 1) its war score cost in whole points, the view's entry keeping it ×1000 (`0x13132b1`); `+0x58` a province for the map's focus, `+0x48` a country's index.

## What the peace view does

- **Its list** (`0x1306a60`) makes an entry (`0x13124f0`) for each term of the tab's treaty's `+0x88` in the category its icons pick, leaving out a term whose `+0x78` answers kind 0 and nothing else: gold, which the view sets with its cash buttons instead.
- **An entry's select box** (`0x1313e70`): a chosen term is taken out with `0x97a8d0`(treaty, term, 0, 0); otherwise `0x97a1c0`(treaty, term, list, 0, 0) asks whether it may be chosen and, when it may, `0x97a790`(treaty, term, 0, 0) chooses it; then the view refreshes (`0x1309120`). The entry's update (`0x1312e50`) disables the box when `0x97a1c0` says no, and its tooltip (`0x1312710`, `0x1312dbe`) passes a `std::string` as `0x97a1c0`'s fifth argument, which gets why not, the term's own description (`+0x108`) shown when it may. `list` is a `std::vector` of 16-byte items the caller frees without destroying them.
- **The gold buttons** (`0x130f3a0`, `0x130f4d0`, through `0x1311810`): the treaty's gold `0x97b2c0`(treaty, out) plus or minus a step, kept between 0 and the gold all the war score buys, `0x97b530`(treaty, out, &100000) (`0x1311869`: 100 points ×1000), rounded to a tenth of a ducat (`0x13118a5`: ±50, then to a multiple of 100 thousandths), then set with `0x97b390`(treaty, &amount), which changes the amount of the chosen `po_gold` term (found with `0x97b8a0`, kind 0): the term's `+0x118`(term, treaty `+0xe8`, the treaty's demand flag `+0x20`, 1) takes its effect off the treaty, `+0x30` gets the amount, and `+0x118` with 0 puts it on again (`0x97b417`). Tested: in a treaty the framework made, `0x97b390` found no gold term and changed nothing; doing what it does with the chosen term changed the cost (100, 300 and 860 ducats cost 3, 9 and 25 against England, whose most was 860).
- **The war score cost** the view shows (`offer_score`, `0x130be13`) is `0x979060`(treaty, 1): `+0xf0`, corrected for each kind's chosen terms whose cost (`+0x120`) passes its cap (`+0x130`), rounded to whole points.
- **The send button** (`0x130e4df`): a peace offer `0x598af0`(action, player, enemy, the tab, the tab's treaty, today) asked `+0x88` and `+0x90` with a `std::string`, as diplomacy's checks are ([diplomacy.md](diplomacy.md)); its tooltip is their reasons.
- **The accept and decline icons** (`0x130c280`): a peace offer as above on the stack, its `+0x120` (`0x59bee0`) asked with a factors vector; `terms_accepted` shows at 1 or more (`better_offer_icon` too when the tab is the demand tab and `0x984020` says so), `terms_declined` below. Their tooltip (`0x130e7a3`) asks `0x4ea1e0`(action, factors) and writes the factors with `0x4e8d90`.

## Whether the AI accepts, and why

- **`0x4ea1e0`**(action, factors), every diplomatic action's `+0xb8`: the action's `+0x120`(action, factors, factors given) at 1 or more. For a peace offer `+0x120` is `0x59bee0`: 99999 when the other country is the player's own (a switch at `[world + 0x23a1]`), 1000 when the offer's `+0x208` is set and no factors are asked, and otherwise `0x1bf150`, the AI's weighing of the treaty, with the factors.
- **A factor** (`0x30` bytes; the vector's destructor `0x4183f0` destroys each `std::string` at `+8`): `+0` its value (i32), `+8` its text as the tooltip writes it, `+0x28` a byte the tooltip groups by. The tooltip (`0x4e8db0`) writes each with its value and the sums under `DIPLO_POSITIVE` and `DIPLO_NEGATIVE`.
- **What the framework answers** (`acceptance`): `accepts` from `0x4ea1e0`, each factor's text (as plain Unicode) and value, and their sum as `total`.

## Calls to arms in a war going on

- **The diplomacy panel** lists its actions with `0x35c230`(country, other, list): for each type, `0x35c150`, which asks `0x35a8f0`(country, type, other's tag, the country's relation record to the other, shown, scripted) whether the panel offers it and makes it with `0x35a880`(country, type, other's tag, shown, scripted), on the heap through the action factory `0x35d210`. A call to arms is type `0x294d` (`callaction`, vtable `0x1c800b0`); the factory makes it with `versus` `---` and `call_ally_type` 1, which `0x53d710` takes for every war the player could call the ally into. Its click (`0xf0acd0`, `0xf0afb6`) opens the "CallAllyDialog" (the default diplomacy window, `0xedef60`, with a promise land box).
- **Its checks**: `+0x90` is the base's `0x4e9b10`; `+0x88` (`0x5426d0`) adds the base's (`0x4e9d00`) and the AI's answer, `AI_WILL_NOT_ACCEPT_CALL` when it would decline, and appends `CALLDESC` (`0x542831`). Its own conditions (`+0x80`, `0x541ef0`): fewer allies than wanted (`HAVE_LESS_ALLIES_THAN`), a subject calling a subject or its overlord, a truce forbidding an offensive war, already called (`ALREADY_CALLED_TO_ARMS`).
- **What the framework does**: takes the call from the diplomacy panel's list as every panel action is taken ([diplomacy.md](diplomacy.md#the-diplomacy-panel)), asks `+0x90` and `+0x88`, and sends it through the command layer; the ledger answers what the ally answered. `diplomacy.options` gives its `Choice` and the ally's `acceptance` as the panel's entry asks it, with the call's byte `+0x54` set. Until A6 the framework made the call with the panel's maker `0x35a880` after asking `0x35a8f0`, and asked its acceptance through `0x4ea1e0` without that byte.

## Handing over occupied provinces

- **The province view's button** `transfer_occupation_button` opens the transfer occupation window (`transfer_occupation_window`, its allies list `eligitable_allies`, `0x136d670`).
- **The check** `0x3dbce0`(country, province, allies, reasons): when the country does not control the province, `TRANSFER_NOT_CONTROLLER`; under siege, `PROVINCE_TRANSFER_UNDER_SIEGE`; with no ally to take it, `PROVINCE_TRANSFER_NO_VALID`; otherwise true, with `TRANSFER_VALID_ALLIES` and the list in `reasons`. `allies` is a list of `0x20`-byte nodes, the first at its `+0`, each with the ally's tag at `+0` and the next at `+0x10`, which the caller frees with `operator delete` (`0x2a5a00`).
- **The command** `transfer_controller_command` (token `0x300d`, vtable `0x1c55808`; write `0x2a5690`): `country` the ally to take it (`+0x50`), `province` (`+0x60`), `commander` the country handing it over (`+0x58`). Its check (`0x2a5950`) asks `0x3dbce0` for the commander and finds `country` among the allies; its execute (`0x2a5630`) sets the province's controller to `country` (`0xa015c0`). The window (`0x136dfb5`) makes one for each province selected with the ally picked and the player as the commander.

## The events

- **War declared, peace made**: the world's wars (`[world + 0x1e30]`, count `+0x1e3c`, [countries.md](countries.md)) are read at each new day while someone listens to `war_declared` or `peace_made`, and compared with the day before: a war object that was not there, or has another name, was declared, its sides' first countries its leaders; a country no longer on a side left the war; a war gone ended, with everyone still in it. A war declared and ended between two days, or a country leaving and joining again, is not seen.
- **Province owner changed**: every province's owner (`+0x468`, [provinces.md](provinces.md)) is read at each new day while someone listens to `province_owner_changed` and compared with the day before.
- No function of the game's is observed for these: the comparison hears every cause alike (a peace, an annexation, a rebellion, the console), at the cost of reading the wars and the owners once a day.
