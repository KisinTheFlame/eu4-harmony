# 2026-11-05 What grows outside the census, and the hidden window concealed (tested by me)

`growth` and `leak` with ten rounds each on master (f29ab09), then M6 of [leak-finding.md](../plans/leak-finding.md), `memory.uncounted`, on this branch. Each run within a claim, the game started hidden; the saves England-1 and 英格兰1444_11_11, new games as France, 30 days.

- **Ten rounds**: both scenarios passed, `leak` with `watch 10`; the runner renewed its lease at each build, and the run of 18 minutes needed no renewal in between. Over rounds 2 to 10, `growth` grew 40.6 MB of private bytes a round, 23.9 MB of it the game's heap, 11.6 MB free pages in the heaps and 5.1 MB outside them; `leak` 46.8, 22.4, 14.4 and 7.2. On 2026-11-04 the memory account had measured 15.5 MB a round, 7.45 MB of it the game's heap ("The text boxes' animations let go").
- **The census did not see it**: the census's leftovers grew 7.7 MB a round (1,235.8 MB after round 1, 1,305.0 MB after round 10), as before, and the sites that leave the most were those of earlier runs (`0x299db7`, `0x155c294`, `0x156acc9`, `0x299e03`, `0x10b59fe`). About 15 MB a round of the game's heap was blocks no site has.
- **`memory.uncounted`, four rounds of `leak`**: marked after round 2 and compared after round 4. What the census does not count grew 30.1 MB a round, as much as the game's heap (30.6 MB). Of the blocks made since the mark, 16,292 more of 3,088 bytes (with the heap's overhead), 24 MB a round, and 59 more of 204,832 bytes, 5.9 MB. The first ask counted the walk's own buffers too (one block of 137 MB and two of 2.2 MB), which it leaves out since.
- **The blocks of 3,088 bytes**: each holds its own address at `+8`, the same pointer at `+0x10` in every one, and `0x400558c0` at `+0x20`. The object that pointer reaches holds the tags `NVDA` and `NVDX`, which among the NVIDIA modules the game had loaded only `nvd3dumx.dll`, the Direct3D 9 driver, holds (291 and 206 times). They are the graphics driver's.
- **With time, not with sessions**: at the idle main menu, three minutes between two asks, 2,395 more of them (13 a second, 7 MB) with the hidden start's sound off, and 22,649 more (126 a second, 66 MB) in a hidden start with sound, every volume set to 0 in `settings.txt` for it. The game process had NVIDIA's capture module `nvspcap64.dll` loaded, with NVIDIA Overlay and NVIDIA Broadcast running.
- **With NVIDIA's overlay off**: the owner turned it off in the NVIDIA App (the registry's `IsShadowPlayEnabled` then 0, Instant Replay's switch 0 before and after, no NVIDIA Overlay process). `nvspcap64.dll` was still loaded in the game, and at the idle main menu with the sound off the blocks of 3,088 bytes grew by 17,157 in three minutes, 95 a second. With the sound off they grew 13 and 95 a second in two runs, so how fast they grow goes from run to run and not with the sound.
- **Shown, they stop**: in one process at the idle main menu, two minutes each, with the owner's leave to show the window: hidden 14,948 more (125 a second), shown with `desktop.show` none, hidden again with `desktop.hide` 2,439 more (20 a second). An experiment build whose `desktop.show` cloaked the window (`DWMWA_CLOAK`) and showed it: hidden 5,476 more, shown and cloaked none, hidden again 3,785 more; the window then counted as visible and cloaked, and the foreground stayed the other program's.
- **The window concealed**: the hidden start now shows the window where Init would, cloaked, as a tool window and without the focus, and `desktop.hide` conceals it so again. Looked at from outside the game: visible, cloaked, no point of three on it hitting it (`WindowFromPoint`), not the foreground, and no taskbar button (UI Automation over the taskbar's elements). Cloaked alone, it had a taskbar button, "Europa Universalis IV - 1 个运行窗口"; with the tool window's style, none. After `desktop.show`: not cloaked, every point hitting it, a taskbar button, still not the foreground; after `desktop.hide` as at the start. 86 more of the driver's blocks in the first two minutes at the main menu, none in two minutes after `desktop.hide`. `screenshot` gave the main menu as drawn.
- **Minimize-all and show-desktop**: with the window concealed at the main menu, the shell's `MinimizeAll` (as Win+M) and `ToggleDesktop` (as Win+D), each undone at once, through `Shell.Application`: the window stayed not minimized, visible and cloaked, never the foreground, and `desktop.get` was answered in 33 to 36 ms throughout, as before them. A minimized game runs at about ten frames a second ([research/headless.md](../research/headless.md)); these leave it alone.
- **Ten rounds of `growth` with it**: passed, each round 26 to 27 s (31 to 45 s before). Over rounds 2 to 10: 16.4 MB of private bytes a round, 7.44 MB of it the game's heap, 7.4 MB free pages in the heaps, 1.5 MB outside them; handles 1.7 a round. As on 2026-11-04 (15.5 and 7.45).
- **What it says**: from the hidden start on (2026-11-04), the rounds measured, besides the game's leaks, the graphics driver's blocks of a window not shown, which grow with time; with the window shown cloaked, they measure the game's leaks again, which have not grown. The leaks the census counts have not grown. Not known: what makes the driver keep them, whether `nvspcap64.dll` plays a part, and whether other drivers do the same. A player's game, whose window is shown, does not have them.
- **Not tried**: the game without `nvspcap64.dll`, the call stacks of the driver's allocations, a computer with another graphics card, Alt+Tab looked at for the concealed window (a tool window is left out of it), Win+M and Win+D pressed on the keyboard rather than asked of the shell, `leak` with the window concealed.
- **Cleanup**: every claim released with every file put back ("every file is as the backup has it"), `settings.txt` with the volumes among them. The runs' reports and the scratch scripts were deleted.
