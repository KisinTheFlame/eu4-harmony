# 2026-11-11 The late frames by what took their time (tested by me)

What the frames late with neither cause in the drawing thread's log wait for ([research/drawing-beside-the-day.md](../research/drawing-beside-the-day.md), "Frames late at speed 5"). The log now counts each late frame by the part of its gap that took the most time, the lock before the frame, the drawing, Present, the lock after Present or the rest, and tells a frame's drawing time. Hidden test games from this branch's core with `dev-test-games`, the 1780.7.1 save in observe mode; the camera moved 8 map pixels every 100 ms by `camera.look_at` where said. Two other sessions' test games ran beside these runs, the computer's load at 70 to 100%: the numbers are high against a quiet computer, and are read against one another.

- **At speed 5, the camera moved**: 80 to 125 frames a second, 56 to 192 late in ten seconds: the drawing 10 to 106, the lock after Present 3 to 47, the lock before 0 to 27, Present 2 to 16, the rest 0 to 19, held back 0 to 2 (the monthly refresh); a frame's drawing 4.5 to 7.2 ms on average, the longest 16 to 75 ms.
- **Paused, the camera still**: 143 frames a second, none late; a frame's drawing 3.1 ms, the longest 9.1 ms. **Paused, the camera moved**: 56 to 108 frames a second, 97 to 322 late; a frame's drawing 5.4 to 13.3 ms, the longest 38 to 118 ms. **At speed 5, the camera still**: 121 to 134 frames a second, 11 to 57 late; a frame's drawing 4.0 to 4.4 ms, the longest 12 to 32 ms.
- **Profiled** at speed 5 with the camera moved (`cargo xtask profile --threads`, 30 s): of the drawing thread's 523 samples running, 406 in the 3D map (`0x10a59a0`), the most in `0x1540c70` (76) and `0x1540610` (64), the scene's passes over its mesh objects, and in `d3d9.dll` (269 with it); 93 in the GUI's drawing; none waiting on a lock.
- **So**: the frames late with neither cause were mostly the drawing's own time, which the camera's moving makes dear, and the wait for the lock after Present, which is the update holding the lock.
- **Not verified**: the same on a quiet computer; a game shown.
