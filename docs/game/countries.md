# Countries

A country is what a player or the AI plays: a tag such as `FRA`, with its treasury, manpower, stability, prestige and monarch points, its technology and ideas, its government and estates, its ruler and heir, its subjects and its wars. The game keeps every tag there is, living or not, and runs each living country through a day and a month of its own; the rest of the game reads a country's state, and much of what it reads is worked out again from that state when it changes. How countries deal with each other is [diplomacy.md](diplomacy.md); their armies and fleets are [military.md](military.md); the AI that plays them is [ai.md](ai.md).

## A country and its tag

- **One array of every tag**, indexed by the tag's number: the world finds a country by its tag through it everywhere ([research/countries.md](../research/countries.md#the-country-array)). Late in a game most tags are not living: in 1780, 118 living countries against 1,380 tags ([research/country-knowledge.md](../research/country-knowledge.md#what-it-works-out)).
- **The game's numbers are thousandths**: treasury, stability, prestige, manpower (whose thousandths are men), inflation and the like are whole numbers of thousandths of their value; monarch points are whole points ([research/countries.md](../research/countries.md)).
- **The three kinds of monarch power** are always in the same order, administrative, diplomatic, military, and most of what a country buys is paid in one of them ([research/countries.md](../research/countries.md)).

## What a country has

- **Technology**: a level in each technology category; the categories, and which kind of power each is bought with, are the game's data, read from its files, so the order of the categories is not fixed in code ([research/countries.md](../research/countries.md#technology)). The next level costs points worked out from the category, the level and how far ahead of its time it is; at the last level the button is gone ([research/power-spending.md](../research/power-spending.md#technology)).
- **Ideas**: the idea groups taken, each with the count of its ideas bought; the national ideas come first and are free. How many groups a country may take comes from its technology. The next idea of a group is the one after those bought ([research/countries.md](../research/countries.md#ideas), [research/government.md](../research/government.md#ideas)).
- **Governing capacity**: the capacity used, and a maximum worked out from technology and modifiers ([research/countries.md](../research/countries.md#governing-capacity)).
- **Money**: a ledger of the month's income in 19 categories and expenses in 38, with last month's and last year's beside them, as the economy view lists them; inflation and mercantilism; and loans, each with its lender (none for a bank loan), interest, amount in whole ducats and due date ([research/countries.md](../research/countries.md#money-the-ledger-loans-inflation-mercantilism)).
- **Government**: a government type with its reforms, chosen tier by tier, the next tier paid with reform progress; a rank, 1 to 3 in the unmodded game, raised at no cost once its conditions hold (development, prestige, not a subject or a junior partner, not held back by the Empire), which gives prestige; three sets of policies, administrative, diplomatic and military; the national focus, which may change again only some years after the last change, fewer the higher the rank ([research/government.md](../research/government.md), [research/countries.md](../research/countries.md#national-focus)).
- **Legitimacy, of the government's kind**: a monarchy's legitimacy, a republic's republican tradition, a theocracy's devotion, a horde's horde unity, or the Celestial Empire's meritocracy in place of legitimacy, each from 0 to 100; legitimacy and horde unity are one field, read as the one or the other by the government, and a personal union's junior partner shows its senior partner's legitimacy. What it adds to the country's modifiers is one of their sets, made again with them, scaled from the value's middle ([research/countries.md](../research/countries.md#legitimacy-and-the-values-every-country-has), [research/modifiers.md](../research/modifiers.md#a-countrys-modifiers)). Beside it army and navy tradition, corruption, absolutism and army professionalism.
- **Modifiers**: about 650 kinds, built into the code and named by keys such as `discipline`, so a mod cannot add one. A country's modifiers are sets of their values, one for what each source adds, summed into the set the game's getters read; they are made again when they change, in the country's day and month. The named modifiers scripts put on a country are kept in a list, each with the day it ends and whether the scripts hide it ([research/modifiers.md](../research/modifiers.md)).
- **Government mechanics**: what some reforms give a government besides, each with its powers, values between bounds that grow month by month and give modifiers in proportion to their value or within a span of it, and its interactions, buttons that cost one of the powers and cool down for some years under a token of their own ([research/government.md](../research/government.md#government-mechanics)).
- **Advisors**: three seats, one for each kind of monarch power, each holding a real advisor or the game's empty one; the advisors offered to hire are a pool of the country's own, kept by the world. Hiring pays a cost once and the salary every month; an advisor may be fired a month after it was hired, and promoted while its culture is accepted ([research/advisors.md](../research/advisors.md)).
- **Institutions**: each institution, once it has appeared in the world, spreads province by province; a country may embrace it once it holds a tenth of the country's development weighted by autonomy, for ducats that grow less the more of it holds, and then keeps a flag for it. The game's check does not ask whether the country embraced it already; the institutions view does not offer it then ([research/institutions-ages-monuments.md](../research/institutions-ages-monuments.md#institutions)).
- **The ages**: the world is in one age at a time; each age has abilities, which a country takes for a fixed cost in splendor once their conditions hold, keeping them for good. The game's check does not ask whether the ability is of the age the world is in; the ages view offers only those. A country enacts one golden era a game, once it has completed three of the age's objectives; it lasts a define's years, lengthened by the months the country gains ([research/institutions-ages-monuments.md](../research/institutions-ages-monuments.md#ages)).
- **Estates**: each with its loyalty, its share of the land and the privileges granted; its influence is not kept but worked out from modifiers whenever it is asked ([research/countries.md](../research/countries.md#estates-and-parliament), [research/government.md](../research/government.md#estates)). Every day each country checks its estates' privileges and its reforms against their conditions, to take back what no longer holds ([research/country-daily-update.md](../research/country-daily-update.md#the-estates)). At most one estate at a time pursues an agenda, started from the game's event offering the estates' agendas rather than by a button; the country keeps it with the day it expires and a scope of its own holding what the agenda picked, and the game completes it once its requirements hold ([research/government.md](../research/government.md#estates)).
- **Parliament**, for a country with one: the issues on offer, the debate going on, the issue enacted; seats belong to provinces. In a debate each seat asks a bribe for its backing, a parliament bribe whose script is its cost; paying one makes the seat back the issue, and the issue passes once two thirds of the seats back it ([research/government.md](../research/government.md#parliament)).
- **Missions and decisions**: the missions in columns of series, each completed or not; decisions taken from the decisions view, each with its conditions ([research/government.md](../research/government.md#decisions-and-missions), [scripts.md](scripts.md)).
- **Rebel factions** against the country: each with its type, name, progress, the country it would make independent, its religion, culture and provinces ([research/countries.md](../research/countries.md#rebels)).
- **Each kind of content is the game's data**, read from its folder under `common/` (ideas, policies, reforms, estates and their privileges, ranks, rebel types, parliament issues, subject types), so a mod's additions are among them and are found by their keys ([research/countries.md](../research/countries.md#the-databases-for-looking-a-key-up)).

## Spending monarch power and money

- **One kind of action for every use of monarch power**: buying an idea or a technology, raising stability, reducing inflation, promoting mercantilism, a general, a core, moving the capital and about forty more. Each is a spending of a kind and a kind of power; the game works out its cost from the game's defines and the country's modifiers, and checks it by its kind (stability only below +3, for instance) ([research/countries.md](../research/countries.md#what-the-economys-actions-cost), [research/power-spending.md](../research/power-spending.md#the-power-spending)).
- **The province view's, the army panel's, the siege view's and the rebels' spendings** (moving the capital or the trade port, a Siberian frontier, tribal land; scorched earth, seizing or burning a colony, attacking natives; sorties; harsh treatment) each have one check of the game's that the spend_power command and the button's tooltip both ask, with the tooltip's reasons and the price among them. The army's spendings carry the army's ID, harsh treatment the rebel faction's. Razing a province is a command of its own, not a spending ([research/power-spending.md](../research/power-spending.md#the-province-views-and-the-army-panels-spendings)).
- **Reducing war exhaustion and strengthening the government** are spendings too, of diplomatic and military points, each checked by one function of the game's that writes the button's tooltip. **War taxes** are not: raising or lowering them is a command that only sets a flag, and their military points are taken each month while they are raised. **Bankruptcy** and the **native policy** are commands of the country alone, each with a check of the view's that writes why not ([research/power-spending.md](../research/power-spending.md#the-countrys-other-spendings)).
- **Boosting a faction** is a spending of the faction's own kind of points with the faction's index as its value; a merchant republic's three factions share their influence, so a boost takes half of its 10 from each of the others. The panel's button posts it only with points enough, and the check refuses only a faction at 100%, neither with a reason ([research/power-spending.md](../research/power-spending.md#the-countrys-other-spendings)).
- **A naval doctrine** costs its `cost` for every sailor of the country's ships, paid in ducats at once, and the list of them opens only from a naval force limit of 20; the command checks only the treasury and that the doctrine is not the country's, the list's tooltip the rest. **Releasing a colony** (the subjects view's "Give Independence to Colony") is a command of the subject and its overlord, offered for the subject types with `can_release_and_play`, and refused while the overlord is at war ([research/power-spending.md](../research/power-spending.md#the-countrys-other-spendings)).
- **The checks are the interface's as much as the command's**: the command that sets the national focus does not check its cooldown, only the court view's button does; the command that repays a loan does not compare the price with the treasury, only the loan window does ([research/power-spending.md](../research/power-spending.md#the-commands)).
- **A bank loan** is the country's loan size times its estimated loan, which follows its income at once: developing a province's tax raised the loan offered the same moment. A country may take bank loans up to a number of its own, at least three ([research/power-spending.md](../research/power-spending.md#the-economy)).

## Rulers and their names

- **A ruler, an heir and a consort** are one kind of person: a given name with the regnal number in it ("Louis XI"), a dynasty, a culture, a religion, a skill in each kind of monarch power and a birth date. Their full name is the given name, a space and the dynasty, in that order, fixed in code; generals and advisors keep one string, given name and family name already joined ([research/names.md](../research/names.md#people)).
- **A country always holds a person in each place**: without an heir or a consort, the game's null person of that kind stands there, and the game asks the person itself whether it is one before it saves it ([research/names.md](../research/names.md#people)).
- **A new heir's name is drawn from the country's list** by its weights; the game's arithmetic overflows when it weighs both sexes, so the list's first name is drawn about half the time, and the heir takes that name's sex then too ([research/names.md](../research/names.md#picking-a-name-from-a-list)).
- **Cultures and culture groups** are the game's data; the East Asian groups are those whose people put the family name first, which the game's full names do not ([research/names.md](../research/names.md#cultures)).
- **A country's cultures**: one primary culture, and the cultures it accepts: the primary one, those of its group the game accepts with it, and those it promoted, each taking one of a number of accepted culture slots. The government view's cultures window makes a culture the primary one, promotes a culture to accepted and demotes one, each for diplomatic points and each with the game's own check and reasons ([research/power-spending.md](../research/power-spending.md#cultures)).

## Religion

- **A country's state religion decides what its religion view offers**: Hinduism and the Norse faith a personal deity, Fetishism and the Dreamtime a cult, the Protestants church aspects, the Reformed fervor, Confucianism harmonization, the Orthodox icons and their patriarch, and so on, each the religion's own. Every one of them is a command of its own, posted by the view's button with the country and the content key of what is chosen ([research/religion.md](../research/religion.md#the-religion-views-commands)).
- **A deity or a cult lasts until the monarch changes**: a deity may be changed at any time but during a regency, a cult may be embraced only while none is followed and not during a regency ([research/religion.md](../research/religion.md#personal-deities)).
- **A Protestant church keeps church power and three slots of aspects**: an aspect is taken into a free slot for its cost in church power, and given up only while stability is high enough; both are the one command `change_church_aspect`, which the view posts with an operation (take, give up, or replace in a slot). The aspects a church may choose are its religion's own list, and a religion's blessings (the Copts', the Zoroastrians') another, which the holy sites the country's religion keeps decide ([research/religion.md](../research/religion.md#church-aspects-and-blessings)).
- **A religion has one defender of the faith at a time**: a country claims it for ducats, with more prestige than the one who defends it, a ruler the religion allows and no regency, and once it has been claimed not again until a date; the world keeps who defends each religion ([research/religion.md](../research/religion.md#the-defender-of-the-faith)).
- **The commands' own checks leave out the deity's and the cult's conditions**: the view asks them, and the framework with it, as the deity's `potential` and `trigger` and the cult's `allow` say ([research/religion.md](../research/religion.md#cults)).
- **Religious reforms are ideas**: a reform group (Nahuatl, Inti, Mesoamerican) is laid out as an idea group, bought through the spend_power command as an idea is, at no cost in the unmodded game, in any order; the country keeps which of its groups' reforms are passed, and the religion is reformed once all are and there is someone to learn from ([research/religion.md](../research/religion.md#religious-reforms)).
- **Fervor effects are turned on and off by one command**, which turns an effect on for its cost in fervor, once, or off when it is on; several may be on at once, and the view offers those whose `potential` holds ([research/religion.md](../research/religion.md#fervor)).
- **Harmonization's candidates are the religions of the country's own provinces**, those of other groups standing for their whole group, less those harmonized already; the check asks the same list the view shows ([research/religion.md](../research/religion.md#harmonization)).
- **An Orthodox icon costs a tenth of the patriarch authority**, which consecrating a metropolitan in a state of 30 development raises; both are commands of their own ([research/religion.md](../research/religion.md#icons-and-metropolitans)).
- **Piety runs from Mysticism to Legalism**, and each end has an action of its own, once every few years, with piety far enough toward it: Legalism's lowers corruption, Mysticism's gives manpower, and either moves piety halfway back toward the other end ([research/religion.md](../research/religion.md#piety)).
- **The papacy's actions cost papal influence, and the curia's buttons are commands of their own**: the papal actions of the Catholic papacy (blessing the ruler, levying church taxes and the rest) cost papal influence, some of them the curia's treasury too, and none of their commands checks the cost, which the view does; calling the ecumenical council and buying indulgence cost ducats, investing papal influence makes a country the next curia controller, and the curia controller enacts one golden bull from the curia's treasury ([research/religion.md](../research/religion.md#the-papacy-and-the-curia)).

## Subjects

- **What kind of subject a country is** (a vassal, a personal union, a tributary, an appanage, 26 kinds in the unmodded game, more with mods) is not kept in the country but in the relation between overlord and subject; the country keeps its overlord and its list of subjects ([research/countries.md](../research/countries.md#a-countrys-snapshot-countriessnapshot)).
- **An overlord deals with its subjects through 41 subject interactions**, a fixed set in code (placating rulers, seizing territory, sending officers, siphoning income and the rest): the subject's type allows each or not, and an interaction that goes on is stopped by sending it again ([research/subjects.md](../research/subjects.md#the-interactions)). Annexing and integrating are diplomatic actions ([diplomacy.md](diplomacy.md)).
- **A subject relation that ends** (an overlord annexed or destroyed, independence, a release) walks the subject's list of the countries it shares a support of independence with, and refreshes the subject's knowledge inside that walk, which clears and rebuilds the very list being walked: the walk then goes on through freed memory or another list, and the game can crash. The list also mixes both directions, the countries supporting the subject's independence and those whose independence it supports, so ending one relation ends a support the subject gave ([research/dependency-teardown.md](../research/dependency-teardown.md)).

## What a country knows of itself

- **Seventy facts kept ready**: the counts of the country's provinces by kind, averages over them (unrest, autonomy), its allies and other relations as lists, its neighbors and border, its leaders, buildings and religions counted. Scripts, the AI and the interface read these instead of walking the country's provinces and relations again ([research/country-knowledge.md](../research/country-knowledge.md#what-it-works-out)). So the lists of allies, guarantees and the like that the rest of the game reads ([research/countries.md](../research/countries.md#a-countrys-snapshot-countriessnapshot)) are worked out from the country's relations, not kept for themselves.
- **They are worked out again** (`0x93fe30`) at once, from 228 places in the game's code (a diplomatic action refreshes both countries), or once a day in the country's daily update when something has asked for it. Late in a game there are about twelve refreshes a game day, of all the countries together ([research/country-knowledge.md](../research/country-knowledge.md#when-it-runs)).

## A country's day and month

- **Each country's daily update**, one country after another on the main thread, is its simulation for the day: its units' days, its knowledge refreshed when asked, the AI's sums of its strength, its estates and reforms checked, its modifiers made again ([research/country-daily-update.md](../research/country-daily-update.md), [day.md](day.md)).
- **Each country's month**, on a month's first day: the AI's budget, the modifiers made again, cores lost that were neither owned nor fought over for years, mercenary companies no longer available, triggered modifiers gained and lost, its units' month. It draws from the game's one random generator, so the countries' months run in their order ([research/monthly-update.md](../research/monthly-update.md#a-countrys-month), [day.md](day.md)).

## Wars and coalitions

- **The world keeps the wars going on and those ended.** A war has its name, its attackers and defenders, the first of each side its leader, and its participants ([research/countries.md](../research/countries.md#wars)). How wars begin and end is [diplomacy.md](diplomacy.md); their battles [military.md](military.md).
- **A coalition** is a list of members kept on the country it is against. A country joins in its daily update, on the main thread; the AI takes countries off a coalition as it weighs what another country means to it, on the game's worker threads, up to four at once, and nothing locks the list, so two threads unlinking together corrupt the game's heap ([research/countries.md](../research/countries.md#a-coalition)).

## Achievements

- **One check decides whether achievements can be earned**: not multiplayer, after a load, the player's country the one the achievements in progress were begun with, the save's checksum matched, the major DLCs, the game's checksum the unmodded game's, ironman, and the game's settings (difficulty at least normal, no custom nations beyond one, no custom development, lucky nations off, end-game tags not blocked). Nothing in it is about the game console, the start date or mods as such: a mod changes the game's checksum ([research/achievements.md](../research/achievements.md#the-check)).
- **It is asked whenever its answer is wanted**, and keeps nothing; but the list of achievements in progress is built only as a session begins, so a change in the middle of one counts from the next ([research/achievements.md](../research/achievements.md#who-asks)).
- **Once off, they stay off**: a game played once with the game's checksum not the unmodded one, or loaded once from a modified save, salts every save after it so that it fails the save checksum. And a save loaded in a session failed that checksum in the unmodded game whatever it held ([research/achievements.md](../research/achievements.md#who-asks), [program.md](program.md)).

## A country's armies defeated

- **A land battle is decided in its daily update**, and a lost one either sends the loser's units off in retreat or overruns and destroys them; an army that lost and retreats, one that was destroyed, one taken out of a siege or by a script all leave the same traces, so the game holds no single fact that says a country's army was defeated ([research/defeat.md](../research/defeat.md), [military.md](military.md)).

## What the framework relies on

- The country array and a country's tag, its snapshot (treasury, manpower, stability, prestige, points, allies, subjects and their types, rivals, wars) and the fields of the player-actions reads, all dependency points ([plans/remote-control.md](../plans/remote-control.md), [plans/player-actions.md](../plans/player-actions.md)); its ruler, heir and consort, legitimacy and the values every country has, its modifiers and named modifiers ([plans/mod-capabilities.md](../plans/mod-capabilities.md)).
- The power spendings, the national focus, loans, ideas, policies, reforms, rank, government mechanics, advisors, estates, parliament, decisions and missions as the panels' buttons send them, with the game's own checks and reasons ([plans/player-actions.md](../plans/player-actions.md), [research/power-spending.md](../research/power-spending.md), [research/government.md](../research/government.md), [research/advisors.md](../research/advisors.md); [plans/mod-capabilities.md](../plans/mod-capabilities.md)).
- The subject interactions and rivals ([research/subjects.md](../research/subjects.md)).
- Repairs: the coalition list's joins and leaves take turns ([research/countries.md](../research/countries.md#a-coalition)); a subject relation's end refreshes its subject once, after the walk ([research/dependency-teardown.md](../research/dependency-teardown.md#the-repair)); a name drawn by its weights; the in-game load keeps achievements ([research/names.md](../research/names.md#picking-a-name-from-a-list), [research/achievements.md](../research/achievements.md#who-asks), [ADR achievement-rules-can-be-relaxed](../adr/2026-11-04-achievement-rules-can-be-relaxed.md)).
- Names in the player language: East Asian rulers family name first, battles named by the language's own order ([research/names.md](../research/names.md#what-the-framework-does)).
- An own army defeated, heard at the two settlements of a battle ([research/defeat.md](../research/defeat.md#what-the-framework-hears)).
